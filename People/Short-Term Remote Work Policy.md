# Introduction

WPP recognizes the importance of balancing the provision of flexible work arrangements for our employees with the management of costs, risks and operational implications of these arrangements for the company. In that spirit, we have developed this Short-Term Remote Work Policy and corresponding process to address employee requests to work temporarily in a country other than their country of employment for personal reasons. 

While we strive to structure the working environment as flexibly as reasonably possible, this flexibility must be compliant with laws, regulations and, of course, compatible with the business need. It must be understood that cross-border remote work differs from a remote working arrangement within the same country of employment. When individuals undertake working arrangements in a country other than their country of employment, there are regulatory implications for both the employer and the employee. Similar considerations apply to cross-state remote working within the US (please refer to the separate guidance).  

Managing remote work within a consistent set of policy guidelines is key to accounting for compliance considerations and minimizing any potential risk for both our employees and the company. 

We encourage all employees to familiarize themselves with the guidelines outlined in this policy and to reach out to their People Team if they have any questions or require additional support. 

This policy is applicable to all employees globally and may be updated from time to time. 


# Main Principles

As a rule, employees must work from the country in which they are employed. This is important for both the Employee and the Company to maintain compliance with laws and regulations concerning individual tax, corporate tax, labour law and social security.  

Employees may request for personal reasons to work outside of their normal country of employment for a short period. A remote working request will be considered only if all the following criteria are met: 

The Employee may have a **maximum total of 20 remote working days** in countries other than the normal country of employment **in any rolling 365-day period**. For some destination countries the maximum number of remote working days permitted may be lower.  

The Employee may be **physically present** in any country, in which remote workdays are requested, for up to **maximum 45 calendar days in any rolling 365-day period**. Weekend 

days, holiday/vacation, business travel and other leave (excluding maternity/paternity/parental leave and unpaid leave) are all counted as the basis of this physical presence test.  

The Employee must have **legal right to work in the country**, in which remote workdays are requested. Legal right to work is defined as citizenship of the destination country or citizenship within a common group of countries which affords legal right to work in the destination country (e.g., EU nationals requesting remote work in another EU country). Nomad visas and other resident permits will not be accepted. 

The Employee must have a **minimum of six (6) months continuous service** in the WPP Group prior to a remote work request and not be under any Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs), formal or otherwise. 

The Employee must confirm that he/she **will not negotiate pricing or key terms of any contracts or conclude contracts** during the remote work arrangement. 

The Employee must confirm that he/she **will not be meeting any clients or colleagues in ****person, or**** visiting a local office** during the remote work arrangement.  

The Employee’s **travel date** to the country, in which remote work is requested, must be **at least 10 days out** from the date of request.  

In addition to the above, each request is **individually assessed for other compliance requirements and risks** (e.g., income tax, payroll, social security, labour law) to determine if it can be approved or must be declined.   

Note that approval will not be given for remote work in any sanctioned country irrespective of whether the employee is a national or has the right to work. No WPP equipment should be taken into those markets.

Once submitted, the Employee will be notified immediately if the request is approved or denied. Special assessments will be required for requests from company officers or statutory directors.

The HR Contact reserves the right to review the request and revoke an approval, if necessary. This could be for reasons of performance management or due to local policies governing physical presence in the office, for example.  

An approval for future remote work can be revoked if relevant laws and regulations change, resulting in a different compliance assessment outcome. 


A request is always for one period of continuous days of presence in the destination country, during which all or a portion of the days are workdays. Separate requests must be made for separate periods of presence, even if they are in the same destination country.

# Further Considerations for the Employee

The Employee is responsible for any additional personal tax or social security liability that may arise as a result of being in the destination country. 

The Employee is responsible for ensuring his/her personal healthcare and wellbeing outside of the employment location, including arranging for appropriate travel insurance, also for accompanying family members (if applicable). 

The Employee agrees to return to the employment location by or on the date that the approved remote working period is due to end. If, for any reason, the Employee cannot return to the employment location by the date promised, the Employee must notify the Line Manager and HR Contact as soon as possible. The Employee may be required to take unpaid leave in this case. 

Employees should be aware that if they do not disclose workdays in a foreign location or fail to obtain approval for same in advance, it may be considered a breach of their employment contract and will be treated as a disciplinary issue. Unauthorized workdays abroad could also lead to unforeseen personal tax and other local penalties in the foreign country for the individual. 

Similarly, requests made using false or misleading information will be treated as a disciplinary issue. 

Remote work requests should be submitted for individual/personal reasons only. Group requests from 2 or more colleagues for remote work in the same location at the same time will not be approved.  


# Business Travel and Remote Work

It is important to keep in mind that remote work and business travel are not the same thing.  

**Business travel is conducted at the request & approval of the company as part of your job**. Cross-border business travellers often enter the country of business travel under a visa waiver, travel visa or other appropriate route; these come with limitations and/or conditions (incl. presence conditions & permissible activities). Permissible activities might include attending trainings, meetings & networking, but not to do normal, day to day work – that is, the same work you would normally do in your country of employment.  

Business travel at the request of the company as part of your job does not require remote work approval. This travel is normally governed by global and local business travel and expense policies.  

**Remote work is** **a personal request of the employee** to perform his/her normal, day-to-day work outside of the normal country of employment for personal reasons.  

The legal right to visit a country on a business trip or for personal/tourism purposes does not give an individual the right to work for the purpose of remote work. If the job is moved to a country, no matter for how long, the person doing that job must have the legal right to work in that country.  

Business travel and remote work can be combined in cases where an employee wants to stay in the location of the business trip for personal reasons and do remote work. The business trip and remote work portions, however, should not overlap. The business travel portion is covered by business travel and expense policies. A remote work request should be made for the additional time and requires approval per the criteria in this policy.   

While some countries have provisions to allow employees to conduct a small amount of ancillary ‘dayto-day’ work while conducting business travel, this does not constitute approval for a short term remote working request.
