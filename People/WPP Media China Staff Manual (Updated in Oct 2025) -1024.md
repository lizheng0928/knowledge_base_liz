EMPLOYMENT

Employment is subject to the completion of the Company’s standard selection procedures including interviews, tests and professional reference checks. When an employee joins the Company, the employment contract shall be signed by the Company and employee according to the laws of the People’s Republic of China.

Classification of Employment

The Company maintains standard definitions of employment status and classifies employees for purposes of personnel administration and related payroll transactions according to the following definitions:

**Full-Time Employment** — Employees work on Company’s normal, full-time schedule. The term of employment will be fixed, or non-fixed as per requirements of the laws and individual’s situation. The position, job scope, compensation, benefits, etc. are subject to the employment contract signed by the Company and employee.

**Temporary Employment **— Employees work on a special project within a fixed time period. The position, job scope, compensation, benefits, etc. are subject to the employment contract signed by the Company and employee. 

**Part-time ****Arrangement** — Individuals work on flexible hours and are paid with hourly rate as agreed by the Company and individuals. The working hours and compensation should meet the basic requirements of the laws.

The students from college who are in the Internship Program of the Company to gain practical experience are called Interns and they are not employees of the Company.

Working Hours/Working Meals/Transportation

The suggested working hours as follows: 9:00am to 6:00pm, Monday to Friday.

These hours include a one-hour lunch break which varies from offices, it is normally from 12:00 noon to 1:00pm or 12:30pm to 1:30pm. The employees may be required to work different or non- standard work schedule according to the policies of the Company.

According to the nature of the Company's business and the specific situation of the employee, or according to the special arrangements of the department/staff team, the actual flexible working time of each employee is allowed to be different from the above suggested hours of the employee’s work, but the working time should be not less than 8 hours per working day.

Being in the office outside of working hours is not considered overtime. Overtime work should be arranged and approved by the Company/Head of Department in writing.

Employees who end work in the office for the day after 8:00 pm will be entitled to be reimbursed the expenses of working meals up to RMB30 per meal per person and who end work in the office for the day after 9:00pm can be reimbursed one-way taxi fare returning home from the Company. The reimbursements are subject to the approvals from your department head/line manager and official receipts should be submitted as supporting document. However, no reimbursement of the above expenses of working meals and/or one-way taxi fare shall be considered as the Company’s approval for or acknowledgement of overtime work.

Probation

All Full-Time employees will serve a probationary period when they start with the Company. Any exceptions will be subject to the approvals from the management of the Company. The length of probationary period will be set based on the term of the employment contract. Your supervisor will conduct a performance appraisal for you before the end of the probationary period.

All new employees should complete WPP compliance training within 2 weeks since joining the Company.

During the probationary period, the Company may terminate the employment contract if the employee fails to satisfy the recruitment conditions.

Salary Review

Employees are required to complete 12 months of service with the Company before they are entitled to a salary review and the review cycles may be longer for the senior executives as per Company’s policies. These policies may vary from time to time depending on the actual circumstances. The salary review will not necessarily guarantee an increase, and recommendation of the salary adjustments will be based on the performance level of the employee and the final approved amount of which will also be subject to the financial performance of the Company.

The salary review cycles for employees will not be modified unless the individuals are promoted with expanded role. The exceptional review cycle such as for an internal promotion or replacing a departure can be approved by the Company according to specific policies and management discretion.

Performance Appraisal – Professional Development Plan

Other than the Performance Appraisal conducted at the end of the probationary period, your supervisor will review with you regularly on your professional development and your performance against the Company’s requirement.

The PDP (Professional Development Plan) system, or other performance management systems will be used for performance appraisal conducted on each employee by his/her manager/director, and the review should include inputs from both supervisors and subordinates as well as colleagues from other departments that the employee regularly interacts with. Performance needs to be evaluated against the performance goals which are set through prior dialogue and discussion. This gives you an opportunity to recognize your strengths and areas for improvement, and to find ways to develop your potentials.

The discussion also helps you to clarify your current job responsibilities as well as your future with the Company and the requirements to proceed to the next level. The rating evaluation and discussion will be strictly private and confidential. A copy of the final PDP form will be kept with the Human Resources Department as a reference for planning of training and development programs.

Transfers

It is the Company’s philosophy to encourage personal career development. This provides employees with opportunities to transfer from within, either to a different department or different work location.

All employees with more than 12 months continuous service in their current team are eligible to apply for opportunities for internal transferring. If you are interested in any position, you can contact your manager and Human Resources Director to discuss the details. The success of the transfer application will depend on the result of the employee’s interview for the position to be transferred and other business conditions of the Company, among others.

Sexual and Other Harassment

4.1	Sexual Harassment

The Company expressly prohibits harassment of any kind. It is the goal of the Company to promote a workplace that is free of sexual harassment by employees, clients, vendors, or other agents. Sexual harassment of employees occurring in the workplace or in other settings in which employees may find themselves in connection with their employment is unlawful and will not be tolerated by the Company. Further, any retaliation against an individual who has complained about sexual harassment or retaliation against individuals for cooperating with an investigation of a sexual harassment complaint is similarly unlawful and will not be tolerated.

Please note that while this policy sets forth our goals of promoting a workplace that is free of sexual harassment, the policy is not designed or intended to limit our authority to discipline or take remedial action for workplace conduct which we deem unacceptable, regardless of whether that conduct satisfies the definition of sexual harassment.

“Sexual Harassment” refers to the harasser’s “sexual” advances, requests for “sexual” favors, or performing other verbal or physical behavior with the “sexual” connotation under any of the following circumstances:  

the harasser informs, in an express or implied manner, the harassment victim that, obeying or rejecting such “sexual” advances, requests or other behavior with the “sexual” connotation will constitute a part of the employment conditions or qualifications, or will affect the performance appraisal (such as favorable reviews, salary increases, promotions, increased benefits or continued employment) within the employment period, regardless of whether the harasser actually carries through with the threats to alter the subordinate’s terms or conditions of employment; or

such “sexual” advances, requests or other behavior with the “sexual” connotation performed or proposed by the harasser will create a workplace environment that is intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or “sexually” offensive, and will constitute an attempt to use such behaviors or requests to unduly hinder others’ work, or actually have an influence of hindering others’ work.

Other sexually oriented conduct, whether it is intended or not, that is unwelcome and has the effect of creating a workplace environment that is hostile, offensive, intimidating, or humiliating to male or female workers may also constitute sexual harassment.

While it is not possible to list all those additional circumstances that may constitute sexual harassment, the following are some examples of conduct, which, if unwelcome, may constitute sexual harassment depending upon the totality of the circumstances including the severity of the conduct and its pervasiveness:

Unwelcome sexual advances - whether they involve physical touching or not;

Sending or circulating, whether in print or electronic form, literature or communications (articles, magazines or e-mails) of a sexual nature;

Sexual epithets, slurs, jokes, written or oral references to sexual conduct, gossip regarding one’s sex life, comments about an individual’s body, comments about an individual’s sexual activity, deficiencies, or prowess;

Displaying sexually suggestive objects, pictures, cartoons;

Leering, whistling, brushing against the body, sexual gestures, suggestive or insulting comments;

Inquiries into one’s sexual experiences; and

Discussion of one’s sexual activities.

All employees should take special note that, as stated above, retaliating against an individual who has complained about sexual harassment, and retaliating against individuals for cooperating with an investigation of a sexual harassment complaint is unlawful and will not be tolerated by this organization.

4.2	Other Harassment

The Company strongly supports the rights of all its employees to work in an environment free from all forms of harassment, including harassment on the basis of race, color, religion, gender, sexual orientation, national origin, age, disability or any other protected category.

Harassment is verbal or physical conduct that denigrates or shows hostility or aversion toward an individual because of race, color, religion, gender, sexual orientation, national origin, age, disability, or any other protected category, or that of the individual’s relatives, friends or associates; and that creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive working environment; unreasonably interferes with an individual’s work performance; or otherwise adversely affects an individual’s employment opportunities.

Harassing conduct includes, but is not limited to:

Epithets;

Slurs;

Negative stereotyping;

Threatening, intimidating or hostile acts that relate to the above characteristics;

Written or graphic material that denigrates or shows hostility or aversion toward an individual or group because of the above characteristics, and that is placed on walls, bulletin boards, or elsewhere on the Company’s premises, or circulated in the workplace on paper or electronically.

Any violation of the Company’s harassment policy should be reported immediately to either your manager, or your Human Resources Director without fear of reprisal. All situations will be treated confidentially to the maximum extent possible and be promptly investigated. The Company prohibits any form of retaliation against an employee for filing a bona fide complaint under this policy or for assisting in a complaint investigation. If the result of the investigation indicates that corrective action is called for, such action may include disciplinary measures up to and including immediate termination of the employment of the offender.

Intellectual Property Rights

Intellectual property (IP) is a term referring to a number of distinct types of creations of the mind for which a set of exclusive rights are recognized under the corresponding fields of law. Under intellectual property law, owners are granted certain exclusive rights to a variety of intangible assets, such as musical, literary, and artistic works; discoveries and inventions; and words, phrases, symbols, and designs. Common types of intellectual property rights include copyrights, trademarks, patents, industrial design rights and trade secrets in some jurisdictions.

Therefore, all employees must ensure compliance with legislative, regulatory, and contractual requirements on the use of material in respect of which there may be intellectual property rights and on the use of proprietary software products in their activities.

Acquiring materials only through known and reputable sources, to ensure that copyright is not violated;

Maintaining appropriate asset registers, and identifying all assets with requirements to protect intellectual property rights;

Maintaining proof and evidence of ownership of licenses, master disks, manuals, etc.;

Maintaining appropriate license conditions;

Emergencies - Fire

In case of fire, the building management has an established Fire Safety Plan that assures the systematic, safe and orderly evacuation of the Company’s employees. Our concern is to make everyone fully aware of what to do in case of fire. The procedures to follow and the method of exit should be as follows:

Evacuate your area immediately when there is a fire or when you are instructed to do so by your fire warden or the emergency communications system;

Walk quickly - do not run - to the nearest fire stairs location;

Do not use the elevators as a means of exit. They can stall because of power failure or for other reasons and become traps;

Do not use internal stairwells (the stairs we use for day-to-day traveling between floors). These stairs may be used when you are instructed to do so by your warden;

Use the fire stairs;

Do not be panic - remain calm - wait for help, if necessary;

If exposed to heat or smoke, stay low near the floor;

Do not open doors that feel hot;

Close all doors behind you;

Use warden station telephone to let building management and Fire department personnel know our location;

Know the location of each floor’s local fire alarm box, fire exits and fire extinguishers;

Become familiar with the floor plan by each elevator bank;

Do not go back for your personal property or for other reasons;

Do not prop fire stairs doors open or permit doors to remain open.
