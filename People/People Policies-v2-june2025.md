


# 1.  WPP’s Respect at Work Policies

WPP expects employees, consultants, independent contractors, vendors, interns, and clients to treat each other with the utmost fairness and respect. Every employee has the right to work in a professional, fair and respectful environment, and every employee has an obligation to maintain such an environment. 

WPP’s Respect at Work Policies also include WPP’s Non-Discrimination, NonHarassment and Anti-Bullying Policies. Compliance with these policies is mandatory. 

WPP encourages all employees with workplace concerns (including any concerns regarding possible discrimination, harassment or bullying) to bring those concerns to the attention of their manager, a member of the People Team, or a member of the Legal Department. WPP also has a Right to Speak helpline for reporting complaints, including anonymous complaints. 

The policies apply to conduct that occurs not only in the office but also at any other location where a WPP activity is conducted, such as a client site or at a WPPsponsored event, or during work-related travel. 

This policy covers all employees of WPP and applicants for employment as well as non-employees (e.g., clients, consultants, interns, vendors, or service personnel) while in the course of performing services or interacting with WPP’s employees and working at WPP’s offices or Campuses. 

### 1.1. 	Non-Discrimination Policy

WPP does not tolerate discrimination of any kind and is committed to providing a workplace free of discrimination based on factors including but not limited to; race, colour or ethnicity, national origin, religion, age, disability, military or veteran status, marital status, sexual orientation, sex, gender identity, expression or transgender status, pregnancy or childbirth, genetic information, or any other classification protected by local law or regulation. 

As part of this commitment, WPP will extend reasonable accommodations to employees as appropriate under applicable law. 

Discrimination against employees is not tolerated, whether caused by other employees or by third parties external to WPP. If WPP determines that an employee’s conduct has violated the Non-Discrimination Policy, it will take steps to ensure the conduct is effectively addressed, and any employee found to be engaging in discrimination may be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of the employment relationship. 

WPP is committed to providing a workplace which is inclusive for all employees including those with disabilities or who may require physical adjustments (such as additional equipment) or alternative working arrangements (such as working remotely) to be able to successfully work for the agency. As part of this commitment, WPP will extend reasonable accommodations to employees as appropriate under applicable law in each county. 

### 1.2. 	Non-Harassment Policy

WPP does not tolerate harassment in any forms.  

Harassment is unwelcome verbal, non-verbal, or physical conduct that denigrates or shows hostility or aversion toward an individual.  

Harassment can be sexual or non-sexual and may include conduct (whether verbal, non-verbal or physical) that creates a disrespectful, intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment for an employee. 

It can include but is not limited to; slurs or negative stereotyping; threatening, intimidating, or hostile acts; denigrating jokes, pranks, or other forms of humour; and written or graphic material that denigrates or shows hostility toward an individual or group. Harassment can be verbal, in writing, through social media, can take place in meetings, in person or online, or at social or other work events – and can take many forms. It is not tolerated by WPP, and we will not allow WPP’s IT systems or equipment to be used for this purpose. 

Engaging in such conduct is a violation of our Non-Harassment Policy. If WPP determines that an employee’s conduct has violated our Non-Harassment Policy, WPP will take steps to ensure the conduct is effectively addressed, and any employee found to have been engaged in harassing conduct may be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of the employment relationship. 

WPP may consider an employee’s conduct to be a violation of the Non-Harassment Policy even if it falls short of unlawful harassment under applicable law. When determining whether conduct violates this policy, we consider whether a reasonable person could conclude that the conduct created an intimidating, hostile, degrading, or demeaning environment. 

This Non-Harassment Policy applies to everyone who works for WPP. Everyone – including managers, individual contributors or temporary workers – is responsible for complying with this Policy. WPP does not tolerate harassment of our people by third parties (e.g., vendors, clients, guests, etc.). We expect employees to comply with this policy in their dealings with all third parties, including clients, guests and vendors. 

### 1.3. 	Anti-Sexual Harassment Policy

Our Respect at Work Policies include WPP’s commitment to maintaining a workplace free of sexual harassment.  

Sexual harassment is employee misconduct and disciplinary action will be taken against any individual who is found to have engaged in such conduct, and any supervisor or manager who knowingly allowed such behaviour to continue. 

Sexual harassment can take many forms. Unwelcome sexual advances, requests, or demands for sexual favours and other verbal or physical conduct are all prohibited. 

Conditions might be placed on individuals e.g. promises of promotion if someone agrees to engage in sexual activity with a colleague or supervisor. It may be more nuanced, and someone may be left feeling that their career will not progress unless they act or dress in a certain way. 

Situations are always nuanced and fact sensitive, but WPP will not tolerate sexual harassment. Physical assault is illegal, and WPP will not tolerate touching of a sexual nature; requests for sexual favours; pressure to engage in unwelcome sexual activities; or any comments about a person’s sexuality. 

Sexual harassment can happen regardless of the individuals’ gender, gender identity, or gender expression and can occur between same-sex individuals and does not require that the harassing conduct be motivated by sexual desire.  

This Policy applies to conduct at work and at work-related social events, office parties, off-sites, and client entertainment events, as well as informal employee gatherings. 

### 1.4. 	Anti-Bullying Policy

WPP does not tolerate abusive conduct, bullying or other intimidating or aggressive behaviour. If an employee is found to be mistreating his or her colleagues, we will take appropriate action to stop the behaviour. All employees can report behaviour that they believe is bullying using the reporting procedures outlined above. 













# 2. 	People Policies for WPP and its Agencies

Each WPP agency should have a separate Employee Handbook containing appropriate terms applicable to its business and the local laws in which it operates.  

The WPP agency Employee Handbook should reflect any group policies contained in WPP’s Global People Policies and nothing in the WPP agency Employee Handbook should be inconsistent with these policies. 

### 2.1. 	Recruitment @ WPP

WPP believes in equal opportunities for all, and it is our policy to hire the most qualified candidates, and to comply with all relevant local laws and regulations. Hiring decisions must be based solely on a candidate's qualifications and suitability for a role and must never be influenced by possible inappropriate business advantages gained through a candidate or the person recommending them. 

All hiring must go through WPP’s established People Team procedures. Any candidate referred by a business partner e.g. client or vendor, must follow all normal People Team procedures. Referral fees from external recommendations are not permitted. WPP’s People Team will involve WPP’s Business Integrity Team if they consider the potential hiring raises a legal or ethical concern. 

### 2.2. 	External Recruitment Consultants

Prior to entering a commitment with any recruitment consultant – retained or contingency – where WPP or agency may pay a fee more than US$50,000 per position (where salary is more than US$200,000), specific permission must be obtained from WPP’s Chief People Officer or their delegate; WPP’s Global Head of Talent Acquisition and Executive Recruitment. 

This approval is to assess whether the position can be more effectively filled with an internal candidate, or through WPP’s Executive Recruitment Team. If the position cannot be filled internally, there is an approved Search Partner list where terms with recruiters have been agreed and approved by WPP procurement. If you need to engage a third party which is not on that list, that partner will need to meet certain criteria and go through WPP’s procurement approval process. Unapproved recruitment vendors should not be used. 

### 2.3. 	Diverse Slate Policy

WPP’s goal is to increase diverse representation across all WPP agencies, with the understanding that diversity means different things in different countries. For all candidate slates presented by Recruitment or HR to the hiring manager, a diverse slate of candidates should be considered that reflects the workforce diversity of the relevant country and adheres to local legal requirements. Any sourcing and reporting of diversity data should also adhere to local country legal requirements. 

### 2.4. 	Policy on Hiring from WPP’s Retained Auditor

WPP head office as well as all networks and agencies should not engage in any recruitment conversations with any individual from WPP’s retained audit firm, PWC, even if that individual has applied directly for the role via a public advert (note, this policy applies to any individual currently employed by PwC and any individual previously employed by PwC in the last 12 months). If there is an exceptional circumstance where this general policy needs to be varied, then WPP’s Group Financial Controller must be consulted, and approval sought before any conversations or interviews occur.  

### 2.5 	Hiring of Interns

WPP and its agencies can provide work experience to interns where this is appropriate, based on the business need and capacity. 

For purposes of this policy, an intern is an individual who works for a temporary period to gain practical experience in a particular field of study or work.  

All interns at WPP and its agencies must be paid for their work (regardless of whether they are also receiving school credit), meeting or exceeding minimum wage requirements and in accordance with relevant labour laws. Unpaid internships are prohibited. The terms of the internship, including compensation, duration, and responsibilities, will be clearly communicated to the intern in writing before the start of the internship.  

Decisions around hiring interns are based on the candidate's qualifications and the business need of WPP or its agencies. Please see thethat must be followed in the hiring process.

### 3.3. 	Background Checks

All Senior Management appointments or gatekeeper roles must undergo background checks and two reference checks. This applies to both permanent employees and short-term/freelance staff. The Talent Acquisition Team can share details of approved vendors. If an individual has worked for another WPP agency (in a gatekeeper role i.e. People Teams/Payroll/Finance/Business Integrity/Legal/Tax) a further check before an offer is extended should be undertaken with WPP directly. 

WPP expects appropriate reference and background checks to be undertaken for all appointments regardless of position. Senior management checks should typically involve third party background checks, unless people are moving country and/or payroll, have been employed by WPP or an agency for at least two years and other senior management can vouch for them. 

“Senior Management” includes all members of the local Board or Management team, any Chairpersons, COOs, CEOs, CFOs, CIOs, Creative Directors and the Heads of all significant departments, whether in agencies, regional or global offices or WPP head office. Gatekeeper roles include Finance Controller and SSC, People Lead roles or similar roles where greater verification is needed, such as where cash handling or management of cash is involved. 

For all other positions (permanent and short-term/freelance), at a minimum, People Team Departments must conduct reference checks.  

Agencies may also conduct background checks if they determine it is preferable or required for specific client hires and/or mandated in the client contract. 

All background and reference checks must be conducted in accordance with local laws. In the United States, please ensure you consult your relevant State supplement. 

### 3.4. Employee Internal Mobility

At WPP we believe our people are our greatest resource. It is our job to empower employees to drive their development by offering opportunities for professional growth across our global network. The Career Mobility policy is designed to provide our people with the opportunity to easily apply for positions within the WPP network to enhance their careers. 

All employees may apply for a new opportunity after employment of 12 months in current role and provided they can demonstrate relevant experience, skill qualifications and are in good standing (as determined through line manager and People manager conversations or local reference standards/regulations).  

For roles with regionally required probation periods, employees may apply once the probation period of the current role has passed or post 12 months in role, whichever occurs first. 

An employee may express interest directly to the hiring manager or Recruiter for the role in which they are interested or by applying directly for the role through Career Explorer or via the agency’s career site. 

Initial conversations with the hiring agency’s recruiter and formal interviews with the hiring manager/teams do not require formal notification to the employee’s current manager unless the employee is Executive Level (Executive is defined by: C-level title; Regional or Practice Leader; or major Client Stakeholder). 

Once a non-Executive employee has received a verbal offer, they are required to notify their current manager and People manager so they can initiate transition conversations.  

At that time, they will confirm eligibility requirements (e.g., confirm employee is in good standing based on local reference standards/regulations, that there are no client noncompetes, etc). 

For senior/executive level roles or for roles with substantial client relationships/revenue, it is the responsibility of the recruiter, whether external or internal, to ensure both People Teams are made aware before an offer is extended. In some instances, these roles may be exempt from the standard internal mobility policy (e.g., eligibility requirements, transition timing) to minimize business disruption. 

Managers and People managers are expected to support members of their team who request consideration for new opportunities and should under no circumstances retaliate against them for their decision to apply or accept another offer.  

If the employee is offered the role, the compensation package should be based on the hiring salary range for the new role, local market conditions, benchmark data, and employment and tax laws.  

In order to ensure that the agencies have sufficient data to make informed compensation decisions, the agency offering should seek WPP Compensation and Benefits guidance on any employee that is being offered, to ensure the new compensation is appropriate and equivalent in all the circumstances of the transfer. 

The treatment in terms of current STIP and previously granted/ in flight LTIP awards for the transferring employee should be agreed at the point of offer and the transferring employee should not be disadvantaged by the transfer and remains eligible for STIP for the period in original agency, and to the vesting of the previously granted/ in flight LTIP awards.  

If more than one agency is making an offer to an employee, they are not permitted to out-bid each other. The current agency has 5 business days to present the employee with a counteroffer that’s equivalent (not over). The employee is encouraged to accept the agency and role best suited for their career goals, regardless of compensation. 

If the employee accepts the position, the employee, current manager/ People manager, and hiring manager/People manager will need to agree on a transition period.

### 3.5. 	Long-Term Change of Employment Location

All employees should live and work from the country in which they are employed. We recognize, however, that our people at times require more flexibility where they work to accommodate special circumstances. Any request for working outside of the country in which they are employed should fully consider all cost, tax and operational implications of the proposed arrangement. 

Cross-border employment is not supported owing to the complexities of managing multi-country working on a long-term basis. However, it may be possible to support a change in employment location where the employee is provided with a local employment contract in the destination country – provided that the employee: 

has been employed by the agency for at least one year, 

has (or can personally obtain) the legal right to work in the location, 

is placed on a local contract with an entity within the same operating group, incorporating all relevant local terms and conditions, benefits and pensions, 

is paid through a local payroll on a local payrate – as benchmarked, and 

has a written agreement prior to the start of any relocation concerning the treatment of commuting travel to the original country should this be required.  

All employee requested change of work locations to a new country on a long term or permanent basis (i.e. not a “Long-term Assignment” or a” Short-Term Assignment”) must adhere to the procedures set out below before any agreement is made with an employee. 

A formal request must be made to WPP HQ using the proper form (available from WPP Global Mobility).  

A copy of the employee’s CV and a Job Description must be supplied. 

A series of questions will need to be answered to review any personal tax or permanent establishment issues. 

The WPP Mobility, Tax and Legal teams will review the information and confirm if it follows the relevant legal and/or policy requirements. 

Additional costs must be approved by the agency Global CFO. 

A written intercompany agreement must be concluded between the original agency and the hosting agency regarding the cross-charge of fully loaded costplus transfer pricing mark-up.  

The employee may not start working in the secondary location until there are formal approvals, they have been hired locally and all pricing agreements are in place. 

The above procedure applies also to external hires who are meant to work in a country other than where the role would normally be based and where the costs are borne. Approvals will be limited to maximum 18 months and require review to be extended. 

In cases where employees are seconded to clients for an agreed period of time, all WPP policies will remain applicable to such employees and any grievances or concerns should be raised to WPP rather than the client. WPP or the agency will resolve the issue directly with the individual and client as required. 

### 3.6. 	Short-Term Remote Work

As a rule, employees must work from the country in which they are employed. This is important for both the employee and the agency to maintain compliance with laws and regulations concerning individual and corporate tax, labour law and social security. 

Employees may request for personal reasons to work outside of their normal country of employment for a short period. A short-term remote working request will be approved if all the following criteria are met: 

The employee may have a maximum total of 20 remote working days (with lower limits for select countries) in countries other than the normal country of employment in any rolling 365-day period. 

The employee may be physically present in any country, in which remote workdays are requested, for up to a maximum 45 calendar days in any rolling 365-day period. Weekend days, holiday/vacation, business travel and other leave are all counted as the basis of this physical presence test.  

The employee must have the legal right to work in the country, in which remote workdays are requested. Legal right to work is defined as citizenship of the destination country or citizenship within a common group of countries which affords a legal right to work in the destination country (e.g., EU nationals requesting remote work in another EU country). Nomad visas and other resident permits will not be accepted. 

The employee should have a minimum of six (6) months service in the WPP Group prior to a remote work request. 

The employee must confirm that they will not negotiate pricing or key terms of any contracts or conclude contracts during the remote work arrangement. 

The employee must confirm that they will not be meeting any clients or colleagues in person or visiting a local office during the remote work arrangement.  

The employee’s travel date to the country, in which remote work is requested, must be at least 10 days out from the date of request. 

More details about the request process and further considerations can be found in the Short-Term Remote Work Guidelines Policy available 

### 3.7. 	Office Attendance Policy

WPP values both collaboration and employee flexibility. This policy – which aims to support a vibrant, collaborative and productive work environment while providing flexibility – outlines WPP's global approach to in-office work. It establishes a minimum of four (4) days per week in the office on average, including at least two (2) Fridays a month, with a maximum of one (1) flexible workday on average. This document aims to clarify expectations, outline the process for flexible work arrangements and exceptions, and address non-compliance. 

**Four-day minimum office attendance **

In most cases employees are required to work from their assigned office (or an approved client site) a minimum of four days per week on average. This reinforces inperson collaboration, team building, client service, career development and connection to company culture. In-office days must include at least two Fridays a month, to ensure consistent office presence for teams and to manage capacity throughout the week. The policy will be applied on a pro-rata basis for part-time employees. There may be some variations from the global policy in certain markets where specific arrangements exist due to regulatory or other considerations. 

**Commutable distance **

Employees must reside within a reasonable commutable distance from their assigned office. Where you live is a personal responsibility and not grounds for an exception to this policy. 

**One flexible workday **

Employees have one flexible workday per week on average. The specific day and arrangements for the flexible workday will be determined in consultation with the employee's manager, considering team needs and individual circumstances. 

Managers will be responsible for coordinating their teams' in-office days to maximise collaboration and team interaction.  

**Flexible Work Arrangements and Exception Process **

The standard arrangement is a minimum of four days in the office on average, with one flexible workday. For any variations to this, employees must seek a formal exception to the standard arrangement by submitting a Flexible Working Request to their direct manager and People Partner. This request should include: 

**Reason for the request **

Employees must clearly state the reason for the request, providing specific details and context. Note: Choosing to live outside a commutable distance or a general preference for remote work is not a valid reason for an exception. 





**Proposed schedule **

Employees also need to outline the alternative work schedule, including days and hours they would like to be considered. 

The manager and People Partner will review the request, considering the employee's role, team dynamics, and business needs (and in accordance with local market guidelines as appropriate). The decision will be communicated to the employee in writing, with a clear explanation for approval or denial. Exceptions may be granted for specific roles, departments, or projects due to operational needs. These will be documented and communicated clearly. 

**Non-Compliance **

Failure to comply with this policy without an approved exception will result in formal disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.  

**Communication and Review **

This policy will be communicated to all employees and available on insidewpp.com. The policy will be reviewed and updated periodically to reflect evolving business needs and employee feedback. 

**Questions and Support **

Employees with questions or concerns about this policy should contact their direct manager or People team for clarification and support.

### 3.10. Global Assignments – Fixed Term

Depending on the business requirements, Global Assignments can be used in some cases. In such an Assignment, the expected new role should not be a permanent job in the host location, and will usually last longer than three months, but typically no longer than three years in the host location. This is typically used to support a short or long-term project, to fill a vacancy where the Host Country lacks the appropriate skillset with a view to transferring the skills, and / or matching a vacant role to career development of senior employees. An Assignment means the employee is leaving their home country but will be returning at a pre-determined date. 

Arranging compensation needs to be carefully considered to ensure it is appropriate and legally compliant. WPP Global Mobility provides advice and guidance on these arrangements and all Assignments must have approval from the WPP Global Reward Director and/or WPP Head of Global Mobility.  

Assignment compensation is designed to be set at a fair level and to assist the employee in paying for those costs that will be higher in the host location than they were in the home location. It is not designed to provide additional total compensation for employees. Please ask WPP Global Mobility for all terms and conditions in relation to the use of, and calculation of, assignment benefits. 

All new Global Assignment remuneration arrangements should be consistent with written guidelines established by WPP and each network. Key elements include: 

All new assignment related remuneration arrangements should be clearly defined in the offer / assignment letter. These should be communicated to the employee in writing before the assignment begins. All employees must sign their letter prior to the commencement of employment. Depending on the duration of the assignment, a phasing out of assignment related remuneration elements may be included, as appropriate.  

An employee should not be offered any overseas assignment until WPP Global Mobility can verify that the employee can obtain, within the required timeframe, the legal right to work (a work visa, and/or working permit) in the country where they are expected to be employed. 

No employee will be allowed to work in the new location unless all immigration and work documentation is presented to WPP and can be verified. 

Given the complexity on taxation in dual locations, to support compliance by the individual and the agency, WPP must provide support to the employee with the submission of their home and host tax returns for each year on assignment and this support can be extended, as necessary. It is most important that each employee follows the guidance and provides information to ensure annual tax returns can be submitted with the support of the agency designated tax consultant.  

More information can be obtained from WPP Global Mobility. 

### 3.10. Remuneration of Senior Executives

It is important that we have competitive compensation at all levels of the organisation. To support the achievement of this in a fair and appropriate way, for senior colleagues, WPP has oversight of all executives above specific salary thresholds.  

To this end salaries, other contractual remuneration and employment terms (including notice periods, restrictive covenants and benefits) for employees with an annual salary of US$300,000 or more in the UK and US or US$250,000 equivalent or more for all other countries, must be reviewed and approved by the WPP Global Reward Director or VP Executive Compensation. This is in addition to the approval that should be given by WPP for agency senior roles - see Employment Offers – Compensation. 

### 3.11. Salary

Compensation is agreed for all people based on their role, responsibilities, experience, qualifications, location (taking account of local regulatory requirements) to ensure that it is externally competitive and internally relative. In line with WPP’s Respect at Work Policies compensation is set without discrimination or concern for race, religion, national origin, colour, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age or disability. 

All salary reviews and any resulting salary changes (irrespective of salary level) must be carried out in the WPP review cycle. However, there may be exceptions permitted in the following circumstances: 

local statutory requirements compel review out of cycle; 

new employees are offered an increase in salary after a trial period; or 

key employees resign and an approved counteroffer is approved. 

Where a different or additional salary review cycle process is planned outside of the review cycle, prior approval must be obtained from the WPP Global Reward Director. 

Under normal circumstances, the review cycle for all employees is annual, however, a review does not necessarily result in a change to compensation levels. 

It has been collectively agreed by all agencies that we will apply a consistent approach to salary review cadence across WPP. This includes the principle that all employees will be reviewed (but not necessarily increased) annually, and we will no longer apply a 18/24-month review cycle.   

WPP will undertake salary changes in May and/or November. Individual agencies may determine on which of these dates the main/core review will take place, for example, a main review to take place in May, with the second date left for ad hoc/in seat promotions, or with populations more evenly weighted between May and November. Both approaches are appropriate and can be chosen to fit your agency. 

## 3.12. Incentive Compensation Arrangements

Incentive plans are designed to drive behaviours and reward successful performance for the good of the individual agency and WPP as a whole. It is also a key component of senior employees’ overall compensation packages and is therefore necessary to remain competitive on a total compensation basis.  

The terms of all annual cash and long-term cash incentive plans must be in writing and approved by the WPP Global Reward Director prior to implementation. Please note that Plans involving equity awards are managed under the WPP plc Board approved share plans and therefore are subject to approval by both WPP and the Group Compensation Committee. All awards made under the share plans will be managed in accordance with the Rules of the appropriate plan as amended from time to time. 

The funding of all annual and long-term incentive plans must be within the parameters agreed with WPP each year, to include the agreement of the Group Finance Director. 

The WPP Global Reward Director must always approve any new individual bonus or incentive arrangements that vary from the approved terms of the agency/network incentive plans. 

Prior to payment, the WPP Global Reward Director or VP Executive Compensation must approve all incentive compensation. The form and timing of payments must also conform to the Group’s tax effective remuneration guidelines, which should be obtained from the WPP Tax Department. 

## 3.13. Retirement Plans, Pension Schemes and other Employee Benefits

Long term arrangements bring considerable commitment to the business and to employees. We recognise it is important to help employees save for their future, but it is also key to manage these long-term commitments carefully. Prior written approval is required from the WPP Global Reward Director before an agency enters into any commitment (funded or unfunded) to establish, increase or change benefits in the form of deferred compensation, retirement programmes, profit sharing plans, life assurance, long term disability or health care plans. Any changes to actuarial methods or assumptions used to determine the level of funding under a retirement plan must also receive prior approval from the WPP Global Reward Director and WPP Group Finance Director where this may be material. Additionally, where Mercer is not appointed pension liability actuary any change of current service provider should result in the appointment of Mercer as local actuary. This includes changes to individual employee entitlements as well as agency/sponsored plans. 

Except where expressly agreed by the WPP Global Reward Director with advice from the Global Head of Benefits, all retirement benefits must be provided on a defined contribution basis. 

## 3.14. Other Compensation Arrangements

Residential property must not be purchased, or leased, on behalf of, or from an employee, without prior approval by the WPP Global Reward Director, unless it is a short-term temporary residence as part of an approved relocation.  

Net-of-tax pay remuneration arrangements, where the agency assumes the employee’s tax and social insurance liabilities must not be entered into except where an employee is transferred between countries on a time-limited expatriate agreement, which has been approved by WPP Head of Global Mobility (See Relocation and Global Assignments sections). 

## 3.15. Tax-Effective Remuneration

All employees must be paid from and fulfil their personal tax obligations in their principal place of employment and residence.  

No employee should be paid from a location where they have no business responsibilities or are not located, unless the payment location is the employee’s home country and the employee is on a defined expatriate assignment and prior approval has been obtained from the WPP Head of Global Mobility, or they have been approved to change their work location to a secondary location under the Remote Work policies. All remuneration practices must be compliant with local tax and other regulatory laws in the relevant jurisdictions. No payments (including payment for expenses) should be made without all proper payroll withholding taxes and social security deductions, unless it is acceptable under local laws. If in doubt, the treatment of these payments should be cleared with the WPP Tax Department. 

The net-after-tax (Corporate Tax) cost to the agency must not be increased as a result of changing the form, location or timing of employee remuneration. This assessment should also take social taxes and benefit costs into account. 

Alternative tax effective methods of delivering remuneration in either a home or host location should not in any way restrict the agency’s ability to relocate an employee or vary future payments under incentive, bonus or profit-sharing plans and should not increase potential future severance costs. 

Where the above conditions are met, and employees are provided with remuneration in any form which is not reported to all the proper taxing authorities, or, where such remuneration is provided by an entity other than the employing agency, a written agreement must be obtained from WPP’s external tax advisers that there will be no tax or other exposure to the group arising from the total remuneration package, wherever contracted or paid, except in the following circumstances: 

where non-cash remuneration is provided as part of the standard remuneration package for employees of that agency, for example, company cars or healthcare. However, for this purpose, bonuses are not considered part of the standard remuneration package and must be separately approved by the WPP Global Reward Director, or 

in the case of expatriate employees where specific advice has been separately received from WPP’s approved third party Global Mobility advisor on the individual circumstances of an expatriate’s remuneration package, and the advice and tax treatment have been approved by the WPP Head of Global Mobility or the WPP Global Reward Director.  

Written agreement must be obtained from WPP’s auditors (subject to independence rules) or tax advisers if the location, form or timing of any payments change in any significant way. 

Any known exceptions or problems that arise, including problems arising out of existing obligations, must be brought to the attention of WPP’s Global Reward Director and Group Tax Director.

## 3.20. Workplace Relationships

WPP is committed to providing a respectful and professional work environment for all employees with equal opportunities for everyone. Personal relationships between employees, in particular within the same reporting line, may give rise to a conflict of interest or the perception of bias or favouritism, and therefore need to be disclosed and managed in accordance with this policy.  

“Personal relationship” refers to any emotional, romantic or intimate relationship which goes beyond the normally accepted boundaries between colleagues.  

Where the personal relationship involves those in the same direct or indirect reporting line, both parties are required to disclose this by informing the People Team. If either employee has influence over the other’s projects or performance reviews, or where they are engaged to work on tasks together, then this may also be considered a conflict of interest and should therefore be reported to the People Team.  

Appropriate guardrails will be put in place to minimise the impact of the personal relationship on the agency’s business and to ensure transparency and fairness. 

Please refer to for more information around conflict of interest and related party relationships. 

## 3.21. Employment of Connected Persons

WPP’ s recruitment process is designed to be fair and transparent avoiding conflicts of interest and risks associated with the perception of bias around the process.  

Extending offers of employment, internships, secondments or work experience to the following groups: employees’ family members, close personal contacts, contacts with shared financial interests or persons connected to our clients, vendors or other stakeholders by above-described interests (each a “Connected Person”) could give rise to a conflict of interest or perception of bias and, therefore, must be properly vetted and approved in a standardized process.  

For purposes of this policy, “family member” is defined as a person connected by blood or affinity, including marriage, domestic partnership or civil union. ‘Close personal contacts’ can be any individuals with whom an employee has a relationship that could reasonably be perceived to impair their objectivity in decision-making. This includes, but is not limited to, individuals related by romantic involvement, shared living arrangements, or close personal friendships that extend beyond casual workplace acquaintances, involving a higher degree of personal disclosure and influence. Examples of shared financial interests include but are not limited to joint investments, business partnerships, or co-ownership of property. 

Any recruitment of a Connected Person must be for a bona fide business need and cannot be for an improper purpose, for example as a ‘quid pro quo’ to obtain a business contract or competitive advantage.  

In addition, a Connected Person may only be recruited to WPP via the usual hiring processes administered by the People Team and any hiring decision must be fully documented. Employees who have relationships with the Connected Persons cannot be part of the hiring process and should remove themselves if they are asked to engage in the hiring or decision making. If a Connected Person is hired, through an independent and fair process, they should not be placed in the same direct or indirect reporting line as the employee who has a relationship with the Connected Person. 

Relevant Annual Certificates of Compliance must be completed annually and as required between annual cycles, and the agency Chief People Officer should be informed of hiring of Connected Persons. Please refer tofor further guidance. 



## 3.22. Workplace Health and Safety

WPP is committed to providing a safe and productive work environment for all employees. This policy applies to all WPP campuses. 

#### 3.22.1. Safe Workplace Policy

WPP will not tolerate any acts or threats of physical violence, including intimidation, harassment, or coercion, on WPP property or in the conduct of WPP business. WPP will not condone any such behaviour. Workplace violence is any conduct that is sufficiently severe, offensive, hostile or intimidating to cause an individual reasonably to fear for their personal safety. Examples of workplace violence include, but are not limited to, threats or acts of physical aggression, destruction or threat of physical destruction of WPP or employee property, harassing or threatening telephone calls, e-mails, surveillance, stalking, veiled threats. 

Weapons of any sort are prohibited in the workplace, at WPP-sponsored events, and at any client or vendor event while on WPP business regardless of whether or not the weapon is legal, licensed or otherwise. 

Any person who engages in workplace violence will be removed from the premises immediately and may be required, at WPP’s discretion, to remain off the premises pending the outcome of an investigation of an incident. WPP will decide what disciplinary actions will apply, which may include termination of employment. 

#### 3.22.2. Occupational Health and Safety

WPP is committed to providing a safe and healthy work environment for employees and visitors. WPP complies with relevant occupational health and safety laws with the goal of minimizing exposure to health or safety risks. WPP appreciates employee participation in maintaining safe and healthy working conditions and adhering to practices and procedures designed to prevent injury and illness. Employee participation in this regard includes: 

Exercising maximum care and good judgment at all times to prevent accidents and injuries; 

Reporting to a manager and seeking first aid for all injuries, regardless of how minor; 

Reporting suspected unsafe conditions, equipment, or practices to a manager or the People Team; and 

Observing all safety rules and regulations at all time 

If an employee reports a workplace injury to a manager, the manager must immediately contact the People Team. 

#### 3.22.3. Drugs and Alcohol Policy

WPP’s Drug and Alcohol policy applies to all WPP employees including at agencies and joint ventures where WPP has a controlling interest. It also covers agency workers, freelancers and contractors acting on the WPP’s behalf or working in WPP agency offices or sites. In joint ventures where WPP does not have overall control, the leaders and managers of those businesses are encouraged to adopt the same or similar standards. 

This policy does not form part of any employee’s contract of employment and WPP may amend it at any time. 

We are all responsible for complying with the and WPP Guidance “WPP Guidance around behaviour at work related events”.  Any breach of the WPP Code of Conduct or guidance could result in formal disciplinary action being taken up to and including termination of employment.  

Substance abuse by an employee can jeopardize the safety of employees and others, adversely affect employee productivity and damage the reputation of WPP. No employee shall report to work or perform work duties if impaired by alcohol, drugs, or other controlled substances. 

WPP does not permit the possession, use or distribution of any illegal drugs, or use of any legal or controlled drugs other than in the manner for which they were properly approved and prescribed, while on WPP or client premises, while engaged in WPP business, or at any event (during or after work hours) organised by or on behalf WPP, its clients or suppliers. 

WPP will make reasonable accommodations for employees undergoing prescribed medical treatment with a controlled substance. 

If alcohol is consumed at a WPP-sponsored or client event (during or after work hours), employees are expected to use good professional judgment, including avoiding excess consumption and/or impairment. 

**3.22.3.1. Alcohol and the Workplace **

WPP is committed to ensuring the safe consumption of alcohol by employees and other guests at work related events or in on-site or off-site facilities around WPP and where the consumption of alcohol is legally permitted.  

The senior management team (usually the CEO or CFO) in the relevant agency should give prior approval where alcohol is being consumed at any work-related event and if approval is given, the management team will then be responsible for ensuring that employees and guests comply with this policy. The senior management team will be required to designate a responsible named manager who will be required to be present throughout the event (the “Responsible Person”). 

Where offices/campus buildings have access to on-site licensed hospitality facilities alcohol should only be consumed in those licensed facilities or be provided by those licensed providers and no unlicensed bar areas or open-source alcohol is permitted. Alcohol should not be consumed elsewhere in those buildings or taken outside, without approval by the CEO or CFO, in which case the policies below will apply. 

**3.22.3.2. Where, when and how can alcohol be served? **

Where an office has no on-site licensed hospitality facility, alcohol may only be served and consumed in designated areas or rooms such as dining rooms, meeting rooms (where no alternative) and generally the preference should be to host an event in a licensed venue operating under protocols to ensure the safety of our people and guests. 

Alcohol should not be served in work areas – no drinks trollies or bars or unsupervised alcoholic drinks should be provided, nor alcohol consumed at desks or in work areas, including in mixed use open plan areas. 

Events where alcohol is being provided should normally take place outside working hours – management teams will need to use their discretion but recognise it is not in our clients’ or our people’s best interests if our people are exercising professional judgments whilst under the influence of alcohol.  If you are celebrating a pitch success or hosting clients in relation to a pitch and there is a licensed provider in an office/campus building where there is access to a licensed provider, that provider should provide the alcohol under its licence conditions and taking into account the rest of this policy. 

Any off-site functions should be held in appropriately licensed venues with drinks served by professional staff and ensuring that they are operating under protocols to ensure the safety of our people and any guests. 

Alcohol should be served for a restricted period, generally no more than two hours – if a function is planned for a longer period of time management should ensure appropriate food is also served and alcohol service should end within a sensible time prior to the end of the function.  Anyone who is showing signs of having consumed too much alcohol should not be served additional alcohol. 

**3.22.3.3. Who should not be served alcohol? **

Alcohol should not be served to anyone underage (as this is defined where your office is located, consider age of apprentices, interns and work experience students in the office) or anyone who appears to have consumed too much alcohol, or in breach of local laws. 

Where alcohol is served, you should ensure that measures served are appropriate and not excessive. 

Safe passage home should be arranged for anyone who appears to be impaired due to alcohol. 

**3.22.3.4. Provision of non-alcohol alternatives **

You should provide free water and make available a range of non-alcoholic drinks such as juice and soft drinks for the attendees as well.  Respect the wishes of those people who choose not to consume alcohol and seek to ensure work related events do not always involve alcohol.  

It is preferable that food is also available where alcohol is served – the management team should use their discretion as to when, if and the type of food that is provided. 

**3.22.3.5. Responsible Person **

The Responsible Person should have sufficient authority and awareness of the risks of excessive alcohol consumption and other safety concerns, to ensure compliance with this policy in respect of a relevant event.
