**BUSINESS ADVISER POLICY **


# POLICY STATEMENT

WPP is committed to conducting business in a legal and ethical manner. Any third parties that WPP agencies work with in connection with client business must be engaged in compliance with the law and on a fully transparent basis.  

This policy applies to the engagement of third parties offering help in any form to win or retain client work (a “Business Adviser”) and must be followed by all WPP employees and agencies. 

In connection with any government business, WPP agencies cannot work with third parties to win or retain client work. There are no exceptions.  

In connection with any other work, WPP agencies are not permitted to use Business Advisers without written permission from their Network Risk Committee and WPP’s Group Chief Counsel. 

Such permission can be obtained by following the process set out below. 

**WHO DOES THE POLICY APPLY TO?  **

It applies to all and any third parties - whether advisers, consultants, intermediaries, ex-employees or otherwise, whether corporate or individual - who offer help in any form to an agency when they are trying to win new or retain existing client work. Assistance can be direct or indirect. 

Examples include:  

■ Individuals who would work with an agency pitching existing or new clients and ask for commission or a flat fee for a new business win.  

■ Individuals or consultancies who would provide specialist advice to clients in particular in connection with new business.  

■ Individuals or consultancies offering introductions to new clients or new markets.  

■ Individuals who hold themselves out as specialist government advisers who can help an agency win new work and assist by introducing government clients or government officials, all for a fee or other form of value. For example: flights, holidays, fully paid business trips, tickets to the Superbowl, entertaining and gifts for friends and family. 

■ Individuals or firms offering subscription services to software that will connect an agency and introduce an agency to new business in different industries using a proprietary database. 

This is a non-exhaustive list. A Business Adviser can be any proposed intermediary or third party who introduces new clients, provides assistance with the retention of existing clients, makes introductions to third parties who could assist with ongoing business or new business wins, or provides advice on particular sectors or business types, or offers to do any of the foregoing. 

**WHO DOES THE POLICY NOT APPLY TO?  **

The policy does not cover:  

■ Relationships and collaboration between WPP group companies.  

■ Most well-established pitch consultancies  

■ Integrated freelancers and consultants whose work is visible and remunerated at market rate without commission or reference to client revenues. 

# PROCESS

Agencies wishing to engage with or renew or extend a contract with a Business Adviser (in connection with non-government business) should email their Network Risk Committee with the following documentation attached in support: 

■ – to be completed and signed by the proposed Business Adviser and the local WPP agency CEO and CFO requesting use of the Business Adviser 

■ – to be completed and signed by the global and local WPP agency CEO and CFO requesting use of the Business Adviser and to include a WPP company reference where the proposed Business Adviser is ex-WPP staff; where they are not, ordinary course references can be sought after approval is given (which, if it is given, will always be subject to references and contract)  

■ Business Adviser due diligence report – please use the WPP search platform Nexis Diligence+ which will allow checks for negative news, ESG ratings, ultimate beneficial ownership, sanctions and watchlists and politically exposed persons – with a cover note to explain how to manage and mitigate any adverse findings (please reach out to WPP Business Integrity for support with this note and any upfront mitigating actions needed e.g. further market intelligence checks through an external and independent provider)  

As part of their review, Network Risk Committees should give consideration to the financial sense and commercial rationale for engaging a Business Adviser and whether the expertise sought is available within WPP including within other WPP agencies, and/or whether the support sought is required on a longer term basis and should be recruited as a permanent role (for example, engaging an individual to draw up and target pipelines).  

If a Network Risk Committee supports engagement of a Business Adviser, the documentation listed above should be sent to WPP Business Integrity along with confirmation of the Network Risk Committee’s support and any recommendations.  

These documents will be reviewed, following which (and assuming all clear) permission will be sought through WPP Business Integrity from WPP’s Group Chief Counsel. Additional questions may need to be asked as part of this process. 

# BUSINESS ADVISER RED FLAGS

The following are red flags and approval will not be provided if any of them exist:  

■ Due diligence gives rise to concerns about the Business Adviser which cannot be managed or mitigated appropriately.  

■ There is reason to believe that the Business Adviser might make payments or other transfers of value that are or could be construed as bribes.  

■ There appears a risk that payments made to the Business Adviser would directly or indirectly (including in the form of gifts or entertainment) go to any client or client personnel.  

■ The client or potential client is part of, or funded by, or has links with a government body (directly or indirectly).  

■ The Business Adviser has a conflict of interest with, including being related to, either the agency or the client or potential client.  

■ The Business Adviser requests payment in an unusual form, or to a different organisation or an overseas organisation or bank. 

■ Documentation does not exist which properly describes the services to be provided by the Business Adviser and the remuneration arrangements. Oral agreements are insufficient.  

■ It is unclear what tangible, legitimate services would be provided by the Business Adviser.  

■ Payments / fees / other value or payment terms are disproportionate to the services proposed.  

■ The Business Adviser’s main location or ownership is based offshore (unless the client or potential client is in the same location).
