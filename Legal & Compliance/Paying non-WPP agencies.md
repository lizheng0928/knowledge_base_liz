Compliance Briefing – Paying non-WPP agencies
Paying non-WPP agencies
1. Introduction
We recognise that being the global leader in media investment means that in certain markets
non-WPP agencies may ask us to help them service their clients and may request remuneration
for the referral. Alternatively, our clients may request that we act as a paying agent and pay
their creative agencies (e.g. via a share of our commission).
Whilst recognising that “there are times the engagement of and payment to third parties is
legitimate”, such arrangements broadly fall under the WPP Adviser Payment Policy. As these
could carry a risk of bribery and corruption, a greater level of scrutiny and appropriate
contractual framework must apply to all such prospective or existing arrangements.
This compliance briefing only applies to cases where OpCos are dealing with non-WPP
agencies. For the purpose of this policy, a non-WPP agency means a ‘non-controlled’ entity
(where WPP has either no ownership or owns less than 20%).
Whilst this policy does not apply to WPP ‘minority controlled’ entities (i.e. WPP owns 20-49%),
the proposed transaction where we pay a fee to such an agency must be analysed and the
analysis documented.
2. Arrangement set-up and monitoring
2.1 Non-WPP agencies as our clients (see Appendix A, Exhibit 1)
Where a non-WPP agency contracts with the OpCo directly to buy media on behalf of the non-
WPP agency’s clients and the OpCo does not have contractual relationship with agency’s end
clients, the OpCo must follow their regular new client onboarding procedures (the Adviser
Payment policy does not apply here). In such cases the non-WPP agency is to be treated as
an OpCo’s client.
A formal contract should be executed with the non-WPP agency with remuneration structure
clearly laid out, particularly regarding any rebates or other benefits that need to be passed back
by the OpCo to the non-WPP agency.
2.2 Non-WPP agencies as third parties (see Appendix A, Exhibits 2 & 3)
In all other cases, the below procedures apply:
2.2.1 Evaluation and due diligence
A rigorous assessment of both the non-WPP agency, their relationship with the client and the
structure and terms of the arrangement must be undertaken before the OpCo agrees to either
(1) service clients of non-WPP agencies or (2) pay a non-WPP agency based on client
instruction1. To document the assessment, Non-WPP Agency Information Form and Non-WPP
Agency Approval Form must be used. External due diligence on the non-WPP agency must
be done unless it is a large, well-known player in the market and there is sufficient public
information available for the OpCo to carry out appropriate checks internally.
1 It is fundamental that the reason why the client is asking us to act as a paying agent is understood and that we are
comfortable that the relationship between the client and the non-WPP agency is legitimate and justifies the payment.

As part of the evaluation, the set up and terms of the arrangement with the non-WPP agency,
including remuneration structure, must be analysed to ensure they are straightforward,
commercially viable and commensurate with the market (see section 2.2.2 below).
2.2.2 Contractual arrangements and remuneration structure
The key terms of the arrangement must be reflected in the agreement between the OpCo, the
non-WPP agency and the client, including remuneration and basis for such remuneration.
• The preferred method of contracting is to have a tripartite (three party) agreement
between the OpCo, the non-WPP agency(-ies) and the client.
• Where a tripartite agreement is challenging to implement, the OpCo must contract
directly with the end client. A short contract must also be signed between the OpCo
and the non-WPP agency clarifying the basis for payment and outlining remuneration
details, including the sharing % (once required approvals have been obtained, see
section 2.2.3 below).
Consult relevant Agency / GroupM legal if you need support.
2.2.3 Approval process
The approval process for such arrangements with non-WPP agencies must follow the Adviser
Payment policy and is coordinated via GroupM Compliance team. The approval process is
bottom-up and sequential, i.e., all such arrangements must be approved by (1) GroupM Market
CFO and CEO; (2) GroupM Regional CFO / CEO; and (3) GroupM Global CFO / CEO. Where
deemed necessary, the approval from WPP will be sought by the GroupM Global Compliance
Officer.
Please note that only when a written confirmation from GroupM Global CFO is received, is the
arrangement considered approved.
2.2.4 Ongoing monitoring
The OpCos have the ongoing responsibility and must keep their knowledge of such
arrangements current. If information is presented at any time that might have given rise to
concerns if known during the initial approval process of the arrangement, OpCo management
must react accordingly. This may mean terminating the relationship with immediate effect. At
a minimum OpCos must review such arrangements annually.
Regional management must monitor such arrangements in their region in line with the WPP
Adviser Payment Policy and Policy for regional offices (section 2. Use of third parties & the
Adviser Payment Policy).
