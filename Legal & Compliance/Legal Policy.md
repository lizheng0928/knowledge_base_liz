



# Point of contact: in-house legal advisers

WPP has in-house legal advisers located throughout the global business, led from WPP Legal team in London. WPP’s in-house legal advisors should be the first point of contact in relation to all legal matters. 


Agencies should not recruit in house legal staff. All recruitment of in-house legal staff will be done by the WPP Legal team. 

# External legal advisors

Agencies should not instruct external legal advisors directly. All external legal advisors will be instructed by WPP Legal team. You should contact WPP Legal if you require any legal advice, including external legal advice.  

# Misconduct allegations

WPP takes seriously all allegations of misconduct raised and recognises the need to protect individuals who raise concerns in good faith. The Right to Speak channel can be used to report concerns, either by telephone or web submission. WPP does not tolerate any retaliatory behaviour against individuals reporting concerns. Equally WPP recognises the need to protect and treat fairly those alleged to have engaged in misconduct. 


These multiple considerations are addressed through WPP’s zero tolerance of retaliation against employees who raise concerns and through conducting fair investigations where investigative decisions are based on fact and, where appropriate, establishing and monitoring the implementation of remediation plans.  


Any issue, allegation or concern identified in or reported through a whistleblowing report or raised through another channel should be reported promptly to: 


WPP’s Group Chief Counsel and/or  

General Counsel, Corporate Risk and/or  

Head of Investigations 


# Litigation and government inquiries or investigations


No litigation, arbitration or any similar proceedings may be commenced by any agency unless WPP Legal has confirmed this approach.  


Where the expected liability of any such action including any legal costs is less than US$150,000, an agency may deal with the matter directly, once instructions have been given to WPP Legal. If it is decided that a lawsuit or similar proceeding should be commenced, external counsel to represent the agency shall be determined and instructed by WPP Legal. 


If a lawsuit, arbitration, investigation, request for information or documents, or inquiry by a competition authority, government agency or similar proceeding or action is brought against any agency or WPP employee as related to the agency’s business or if any employee becomes aware of facts or circumstances that may give rise to such action, the WPP Legal team must be informed immediately of the following: 


name of the person, company or government body commencing the action; 

name of the person responsible for handling the claim; 

the claims alleged against the agency or facts and circumstances that are likely to give rise to such a claim; and; 

estimated amount of such claim. 

No action or position shall be taken prior to discussions with WPP Legal. External Legal Advisors to represent the agency shall be determined by WPP Legal.  


All documentation must be marked “Confidential: Attorney-Client Privileged 

Communication” and should be sent to the appropriate legal representative of WPP. 

# Legal structure

The legal structure of the WPP Group is complex – decisions regarding the structure to be adopted in a given country or its modification must be made in agreement with and under the supervision of WPP’s Tax and Legal departments. These changes to legal structure or joint ventures need to be approved through WPP’s Group Chief Counsel or General Counsel in WPP Legal.  


No new entities, companies, partnerships, joint ventures, affiliation or licence structures or branches can be created without the prior written consent of the WPP Tax and Legal departments and after a detailed business plan has been submitted to and approved by the WPP Group Finance Director.  


No joint venture or shareholder agreements or affiliate agreements can be negotiated or entered into without the prior review and approval of the WPP Legal team.  


Details of any changes in structure must be notified to WPP Legal at least one month prior to the intended date of change and no change should be implemented until consent from the WPP Legal and Tax departments has been obtained. Changes include: 


changes of name or ownership; 

formation of new units (whether joint venture, branch, partnership or company); 

liquidations of companies/cessation of other business forms; 

any affiliation, partnership, revenue sharing or alliance agreements or similar; 

changes to legal structures; and 

proposed acquisitions or disposals of business units of either a discrete part or the whole (whether by way of shares or business assets). 

# Intellectual property, including trademarks, trade names and patents

No application to register any patent or register or licence the use of a trademark may be made except through WPP Legal in London, which maintains a trademark database. The WPP Legal department has the responsibility of liaising with the WPP Tax Department to ensure that the ownership of all trademarks is properly structured. 

# Proxies/powers of attorney

All requests for proxies from the WPP holding companies should be sent to the WPP Company Secretary at least 2 weeks prior to the date on which they are required, with supporting documentation and full details of the reasons for requesting the proxy. 


All proxies need to state the list of actions/resolutions to which they relate and should attach any documents referred to in the proxy. The period of appointment under the proxy should be no longer than 3 months. General proxies permitting the proxy holder to take “such action as the proxy sees fit” will not be approved under any circumstances. 

# Dating documents

In no circumstances should agreements, powers of attorney, resolutions or other legal documentation be backdated. Any requests for proxies or powers of attorney to support meetings that have already taken place will be refused. 

# Guarantees

No commitments should be made to provide any form of guarantee whether for financial, banking, client or supplier commitments without the prior written consent of the WPP Legal team.
