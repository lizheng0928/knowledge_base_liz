Compliance Briefing – Requests to invoice or pay different entities
1. Introduction
1.1 Background - Criminal Finances Act 2017
The UK Government has introduced new corporate offences of failure to prevent the criminal facilitation
of tax evasion contained in the Criminal Finances Act 2017 which came into force on 30 September
2017. The Act makes businesses liable for the actions of their employees and other ‘associated
persons’ who intentionally facilitate tax evasion. Businesses will be liable even in cases where senior
management were either uninvolved or unaware of the acts. Those found guilty will potentially face
unlimited fines, a criminal record, and will be barred from public sector procurement. This regulation
has extraterritorial reach, i.e. applies to all OpCos, not just those based in the UK.
We must ensure that we never commit an act which is, or could be construed, illegal, including becoming
a party to tax evasion or money laundering scheme.
1.2 Existing WPP and GroupM policies and guidance
This guidance complements WPP and GroupM rules regarding invoicing, payment and working with 3rd
parties, such as:
 Sections 5 and 6 of the WPP Policy Book (Clients’ and ‘Procurement & Contracting’ respectively);
 A suite of WPP Anti-bribery policies;
 GroupM Commercial policies, templates and guidance that are available on the Client Commercial
site.
Remember to STOP – THINK – CONSULT
2. GroupM additional guidance
2.1 Changing parties we invoice or pay
Our default position is to invoice, accept payment from or make payments to the party with whom we
contract (Note: whilst our preference is to always have a formal contract in place, in the absence of a
formal contract, it will be a party with whom we negotiated and dealt with). However, if a client or supplier
requests that we invoice, accept payment from or make payments to a party which is not the one we
have the original relationship with, the OpCo must understand and analyse the following:
WHO?  Identify the new party whom we are requested to invoice or pay.
 Perform due diligence on the new party that is requested to be billed / paid.
WHY?  Enquire as to the reason / justification for the request to make the change.
 Be vigilant and beware of the money laundering / tax evasion risks.
WHAT?  Ascertain the relationship between the contracting party (or the original party we
have been dealing with if there is no formal contact in place) and the new party to
be invoiced / paid.
 Ensure the description on all invoices – with suppliers and clients – properly reflects
the nature of the service received or provided.
 Verify there is sufficient documentation to serve as a basis to invoice, accept
payment from or make payments to a third party.
 Ensure the appropriate protection is in place if the third party defaults on payment.

Compliance Briefing – Requests to invoice or pay different entities
DO NOT:
× Invoice or pay a party (individual or company) unrelated to the party you have contracted with /
provided services to.
× Invoice or pay an individual employed by the company or a shareholder or any other such party even
if a client / supplier requests so.
