**Gifts, Entertainment & Hospitality Guidance Booklet **


It is recognised that giving and receiving gifts, entertainment and hospitality can constitute perfectly proper business conduct in building and maintaining relationships. However, because they can also create actual or perceived conflicts of interest or be given or accepted for dishonest or unethical reasons, even if that was not your intention, we are careful about what is and is not acceptable and compliance with WPP’s Gifts, Entertainment & Hospitality Policy (the “GEH Policy”).  


WPP’s Code of Business Conduct states:  

*We will not offer any items of personal inducement to secure business. This is not intended to prohibit appropriate entertainment or the making of occasional gifts of minor value unless the client has a policy that restricts this.  *

*We will not accept for our personal benefit goods or services of more than nominal value from suppliers, potential suppliers or other third parties.   *

*We will not… give, offer or accept bribes, whether in cash or otherwise, to or from any third party, including but not restricted to government officials, clients and brokers or their representatives.  *


**What to think about? **

It is important to understand when gifting, entertainment or hospitality might be appropriate or inappropriate, whether it was given with integrity and goodwill or with the expectation or hope of getting something of value in return or to influence a decision.  

The GEH Policy sets out some instances where it is prudent to say no regardless of whether there are obviously nefarious reasons involved – i.e. gifts, entertainment and hospitality will be considered unacceptable and not permitted regardless: 


where the law or client contract prohibits the gift, entertainment or hospitality 


this is irrespective of your contact at the client insisting otherwise – if you feel uncomfortable or under pressure with a client please contact your line manager, People /HR contact or WPP Business Integrity for support 


you are responsible for knowing the rules around any client engagement you work on – clients especially multinational corporates will have policies similar to ours which will set limits on what is acceptable for their employees to receive or their suppliers to give – for example - ‘Code of Business Conduct for Suppliers to The Coca-Cola Company’: 


*“Employees of The Coca-Cola Company are prohibited from accepting anything more than modest gifts, meals and entertainment from suppliers. Ordinary business meals and small tokens of appreciation such as gift baskets at holiday time generally are fine, but suppliers should avoid offering Coca-Cola employees travel, frequent meals or expensive gifts. Gifts of cash or cash equivalents, such as gift cards, are never allowed. When providing services to the Company or otherwise **when acting on the Company’s behalf, supplier’s employees are subject to the same limits described in this section when offered gifts, meals or entertainment by Company’s customers, suppliers, or other business partners.” *


where they are given or received around a pitch or key decision – the risk is that there is actual or perceived influence on the pitch outcome or decision making 


where they are given to or received from a government official - there are specific laws such as the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act 1977 prohibiting bribery of foreign officials – see further WPP’s AFBAC Policy and AFBAC Guidance Booklet 


where a cash gift is proposed – cash is inherently more difficult to trace and so can fuel both the actual and perceived risk of corruption 


Additional considerations: 

Is the value of the gift, entertainment or hospitality below or above your agency’s financial threshold – this will have been set carefully to support the argument that anything above has the potential to influence the recipient. This does not mean that anything above cannot be given or accepted but it will need to be discussed you’re your line manager per the GEH Policy. *Example: A ticket to a local sporting event above the financial threshold would normally be acceptable still. Tickets for you and a partner overseas with accommodation to see a sporting event would not normally be acceptable.*  

Is the gift, entertainment or hospitality given to substantially all participants in a market – for example, representatives of all principal media competitors to attend a media owner event? If so, it will be deemed less likely that the gift, entertainment or hospitality will influence staff. If the gift, entertainment or hospitality is available only to our agencies, it can appear as if our influence in particular is sought and specific line manager approval may be needed.  

Who will benefit from the gift, entertainment or hospitality? If a family member will benefit, you must give greater consideration as to whether it is appropriate than if it was you and a colleague or an employee from a client or supplier – i.e. a legitimate work-related event rather than something partly for personal use.  

What would others think? Would accepting a particular gift or entertainment or hospitality package be perceived as unusual or excessive? Would you like to be named in the trade press as accepting (or giving) such a gift or hospitality?  

It will not mitigate the potential risks and may look worse to pay out of your own pocket for gifts, entertainment or hospitality for a business contact. 

If a gift is received that is excessive (for example, electronic equipment, a car or a holiday), you should first try to decline the gift. If this is not possible, the item could be given to charity or raffled for all members of staff with the proceeds being sent to charity.  


**Gift registers  **

All WPP agencies should maintain a register of all gifts, entertainment and hospitality, given or received, above the agency’s financial threshold (set by the agency CFO). The register format may vary (e.g. electronic vs paper) but the following must be captured at a minimum: 

Names, titles and company of giver(s) 

Names, titles and company of recipient(s) 

Date of giving or receiving 

Description of the gift, entertainment or hospitality 

Actual value (an estimate is acceptable where an actual value is genuinely unavailable) 

Confirmation that the timing of the gift, entertainment or hospitality does not coincide with a pitch or key decision 

Confirmation that the giver or recipient is not a government official 

Name and title of line manager who has approved
