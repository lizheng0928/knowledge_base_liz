**1. ****General Policy Statement**** **


We do not condone tax evasion in any circumstances. 

We will not facilitate tax evasion, or allow individuals associated with the WPP Group to facilitate tax evasion. 

We will collectively ensure that all staff and individuals associated with WPP understand this policy through training, communication and by example. 

We will need to fully test the tax analysis of any activities that we feel are controversial, even if we are told it is industry practice in the country concerned and should be erring on the side of caution and regularising any activities that could be viewed as aggressive. 


Tax evasion can involve any tax, whether it is corporate taxes, individual income taxes, social security taxes, VAT and other sales taxes or other transaction taxes.  


Tax evasion can involve the following: 


not fully declaring income to tax authorities; 

incorrectly operating or failing to operate local income or social security taxes; 

presenting a different set of financial records to the tax authorities; 

claiming tax deductions for fictitious invoices, invoices for unsupported services or agreeing to settle a supplier invoice which does not accurately reflect the services or products which have been provided by the supplier; 

applying freelancer treatment to individuals when in substance they are really employees; 

using cash to make payments to suppliers or employees; 

agreeing to pay suppliers into a bank account in a different country to where they are providing their services, in order to help them evade tax; 

operating in other countries without registering in those countries (as a company and also for their employees); 

having companies in tax havens or transacting with companies in tax havens (please see below); and 

undertaking any activities or using structures designed to reduce taxes payable that local tax authorities could assert to be tax evasion. 


# Tax havens


Everyone should be fully aware of any situations where payments are being made to entities registered in tax havens or bank accounts located in tax havens.  


In such situations, we should assess whether the payment is appropriate given the risk of facilitating tax evasion and we should obtain appropriate warranties and representations confirming declaration of the funds to all relevant tax authorities where it is considered appropriate to proceed with the payment.

## 1.1 Preventing global tax evasion (Corporate Criminal Offence (CCO))


# Summary


Under UK legislation, it is a criminal offence if a company fails to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion. This applies to all individuals and companies in our Group as it applies to both UK tax evasion and overseas tax evasion.  


# Criminal prosecution and unlimited penalties


The consequences of tax evasion can be serious. If the Group cannot evidence that it had reasonable procedures in place to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion by persons acting on its behalf, then it could be subject to a corporate criminal conviction and unlimited fines. 


# Reasonable procedures


Group companies are expected to maintain reasonable procedures to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion. These procedures should be fully documented. These procedures will include: 


an up-to-date risk assessment; 

appropriate due diligence processes over associated persons;  

a clear tone from the top that tax evasion and its facilitation are not tolerated; 

preventative procedures i.e., controls that are proportionate to the risks identified within the risk assessment; 

training and communication to employees in order to raise awareness;  

ongoing monitoring and review of any risks identified, and the effectiveness of the mitigating controls that are in place. 


These procedures may be documented at WPP agency or sub-agency level, as long as it is clear which Group companies they relate to.

## 1.2 Notification of tax audits


The commencement of all tax audits whether relating to corporate income taxes, personal income taxes or other taxes (e.g., payroll, VAT or other indirect taxes) should be notified immediately to WPP Tax. The same applies to claims for unpaid withholding taxes on cross-border payments. 


In respect of areas which are considered to be high risk, WPP Tax may subsequently request that it reviews and approves all correspondence with tax authorities as regards the tax audit, including settlement discussions.  


WPP Tax should be notified when tax audits are closed/concluded, even if they are settled without any claims or adjustments. 


No tax audit or other claim of over US$250,000 may be settled or agreed without pre-approval from WPP Tax.  


## 1.3 Tax planning and tax rulings


**a) Tax planning **


All Group companies should seek prior approval from WPP Tax concerning any tax planning opportunities which are being considered. 


In accordance with the WPP Tax Strategy, WPP will not engage in artificial transactions, the sole purpose of which is to reduce tax.  


However, we will consider undertaking a transaction in a way that gives rise to tax efficiencies providing this is aligned with our commercial objectives and complies with the associated tax legislation. We will not transact in this way if the underlying commercial objectives do not support the position, or if the arrangements impact upon our reputation, brand, corporate and social responsibilities, or future working relationships with tax authorities. 


Accordingly, we may utilise tax incentives or opportunities to transact efficiently from a tax perspective where these:  


are not considered to carry significant reputational risk or significant risk of damaging our relationship with the tax authorities in the jurisdictions in which we operate;  

are aligned with the intended policy objectives of the governments which introduced the incentives; and  

are aligned with our business or operational objectives.  


The Group aims for certainty on tax positions it adopts but where tax law is unclear or subject to interpretation, written professional advice or confirmation will be sought as appropriate to ensure that our adopted tax position is at least “probable” to apply. 


# b) Tax rulings


In certain cases, it may be appropriate to obtain a ruling from the tax authorities in order to obtain greater certainty on the tax treatment of an arrangement. Such cases should be raised with WPP Tax before applying for the ruling, as the ruling may have wider implications for Group companies. 


## 1.4 Use of tax advisers


All new appointments of tax advisers must be cleared in advance with WPP Tax.  


Tax advisers (including existing advisers) should not be engaged on significant projects before seeking approval from WPP Tax.  


All contingent fee arrangements with tax advisers must be approved by WPP Tax in advance, regardless of the amount of fees that are potentially payable to the adviser. WPP Tax will evaluate the appropriateness of such arrangements.  


The preferred suppliers of tax advice to the Group are EY, although for historic reasons other advisors may be used in particular countries. If you are unsure who are our tax advisors in your country, please contact WPP Tax. 


If a Group company utilises another tax adviser, it must provide an instruction to that adviser to co-operate with and provide all requested information to the EY office to facilitate international and territorial (as opposed to company only) tax compliance and (if appropriate) advisory.

All tax professionals, including tax professionals employed by WPP agencies and shared services, are required to comply with the WPP Tax policy, including ensuring the prompt escalation of relevant matters to WPP Tax in accordance with this policy.** **

## 1.9 Transfer pricing


Related party transactions fall under the remit of transfer pricing legislation which varies by jurisdiction. In accordance with local legislation, which typically 

incorporates the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and Tax Administrations, related party transactions should be implemented in accordance with the arm’s length principle.  


For the avoidance of doubt, related party transactions include transactions between any entities within the WPP Group – this could be within the same network/agencies or across different network/agencies. Examples of common related party transactions include: 


payments to other WPP Group companies for services of any kind (e.g., business support services provided by WPP and/or Network HQs, WPP IT services, intra-Group client services including work performed under subcontracting arrangements, outsourcing/offshoring arrangements, intercompany lending of staff); 

entering into other loan/funding arrangements with other WPP Group companies; 

participation in a Group cash pooling arrangement; 

any use of intellectual property/intangible assets of another WPP Group company whether or not an intercompany charge is actually made between the parties; 

recharging costs to a WPP Group company e.g., flow through costs, media. 


In order to demonstrate compliance with the arm’s length principle, Group companies are responsible for maintaining contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation on an annual basis, to support the position adopted in the corporate income tax return. This means that transfer pricing documentation should be prepared by the time the corporate income tax return is filed. In some cases, it may be necessary to submit transfer pricing documentation alongside the tax return or at a later date once requested by the relevant local tax authority. In recent years, many tax authorities have shortened the request period for submitting documentation, for example in some countries the submission window is as short as seven days.  


Transfer pricing documentation is considered to be made up of:

Local file: Group companies are required to prepare local file transfer pricing annually. The local file should be based on the local file template distributed by WPP Tax (and available on InsideWPP  and adjusted for any specific local documentation requirements, in order to ensure penalty protection locally. 


Country-by-country report (“CbCR”): the CbCR itself is prepared and submitted by WPP Tax annually, however Group companies are required to make any CbCR notifications locally in line with local regulations. CbCR notifications usually include standard data (available from WPP Tax). 


In addition to the above, depending on local transfer pricing regulations, Group companies are also responsible for preparing any local transfer pricing related forms or returns which support the corporate income tax return filing. Group companies should ensure that related party transactions are consistently disclosed across all transfer pricing related filings (including transfer pricing documentation).

any cross-border transfer of ten or more staff members who are presently employed by the same legal entity. 


## 1.10 Transactions between US and non-US Group Companies


Each related party transaction may have separate tax reporting requirements and may be subject to additional taxes in some jurisdictions. 


As a result, Group companies are responsible for properly accounting for these transactions in intercompany accounts and maintaining appropriate documentation to support the nature of the charge and counterparty information. Improper record keeping could subject the relevant company to penalties.  


For example, in the U.S., any transaction between a U.S. WPP entity and a non-U.S. WPP entity must be reported on WPP’s U.S. federal income tax return.   


Furthermore, any payment from a U.S. WPP entity to a non-U.S. WPP entity may be subject to the U.S. base erosion anti-abuse tax (“BEAT”) at a rate of 10%-12.5%.   


Failure to report each transaction may be subject to penalties of USD$25,000 per non-U.S. entity per year. If these or other similar penalties result from a Group company failing to report intercompany transactions to WPP Tax, such penalties will be charged to the non-compliant Group company.    


## 1.11 Board meetings and need for local directors


In order to reduce the risk of a tax authority challenging that a Group company is tax resident in more than one country: 


the majority of directors should be tax resident in the same country that the Group company is tax resident; and 

board meetings should be convened (with a majority of local directors being physically present) in the country in which the Group company is tax resident. 


WPP Tax and Legal approval is required where: 


the location of Board meetings is to be moved to a country in which the relevant Group company is not tax resident; or  

the composition of the Board is to be altered, with the appointment of one or more directors who are not personally tax resident in the same country that the Group company is tax resident. 


## 1.12 Finance policy, process or system changes


Changes to finance policies, processes or systems can impact the ability of Group companies to remain compliant with tax laws.  


Where a new finance policy, process or system change or implementation will have a significant impact on the Group’s tax processes or tax liabilities, WPP Tax should be consulted at the earliest opportunity.  


Examples of such arrangements include: the introduction of new shared services centres, new billing arrangements for multiple clients – such as new billing hubs, and the introduction of new Enterprise Resource Planning (“ERP”) or other finance systems, to ensure that any tax considerations are factored into their design and implementation. 


## 1.13 Contracting with clients


Group companies should ensure that appropriate tax terms and conditions are included within client contracts. Standard tax clauses are available from WPP Tax or on the Tax page on Inside WPP under client contracts:  


Contracts should, as a minimum, clarify that the client is responsible for making payment of all indirect taxes, including VAT, GST or Sales Taxes, that are applicable to our services.  


WPP Tax approval should be sought on all cross-border central billing or hub billing arrangements with clients, or cross-border billing which exceeds USD$1m where Withholding Tax is expected to apply. For the purposes of this policy, central billing and hub billing are defined as:.  


Central Billing: Where an arrangement requires multiple WPP companies to issue cross-border invoices to a single WPP company, which in turn issues a single consolidated invoice to a client in a particular location. 


Hub Billing: Where an arrangement requires multiple WPP companies to issue cross-border invoices to a client company in a particular location (i.e., the client hub location). 


## 1.14 Contracting with suppliers


Group companies should ensure that appropriate tax terms and conditions are included within supplier contracts. Standard tax clauses are available from WPP Tax and WPP Legal. 


Contracts should, as a minimum, clarify that the supplier must issue a valid tax invoice, as required under applicable law, to the appropriate WPP Group company.  


Furthermore, Group companies should not agree to gross-up payments to suppliers in respect of withholding taxes on cross-border payments. 


## 1.15 Indirect taxes


Group companies should have a process in place to determine whether the company holds the correct documentary evidence to support input VAT/GST/Sales Tax (collectively “indirect taxes”) deduction, where relevant. Group companies should also have clear guidelines to ensure that the correct amount of input indirect tax is deducted, and Group companies block input indirect tax as required by local law. 


Likewise, Group companies should have a review process in place to ensure that indirect tax is added to customer invoices correctly. Where invoices are issued with no indirect tax applied, the Group company must have the correct documentary evidence in place to support this. 


Where errors are identified, disclosures and payments of under-declared or overrecovered taxes should be made to the tax authorities as appropriate. 


As with other applicable taxes, indirect tax returns should be submitted by the statutory deadline and any payments should be made in a timely manner. 


For US Group companies specifically, if purchases do not include the appropriate US sales tax, Group companies are required to self-assess use taxes and pay the appropriate tax jurisdiction the additional use taxes that are due. Please consult with WPP Tax for further guidance on US Sales & Use Taxes.  


## 1.16 Withholding taxes


Group companies are responsible for withholding tax compliance, including retaining the appropriate documentation, such as residency certificates or beneficial ownership confirmation statements, that supports the withholding tax position that has been adopted.  


Group companies should have a process in place to ensure that the withholding tax position on payment is considered upfront when making or receiving payments to/from overseas companies, including Group companies and third parties – such as clients and suppliers. This should include assessing whether the relevant double tax treaty can reduce the default withholding tax rate. 


## 1.17 Employees working overseas


Group companies should not have any employees based overseas for any duration without pre-clearance from WPP Tax, Legal and WPP Global Mobility in accordance with WPP remote working/internal mobility policies. The same applies to any employee hosting relationships where employees are legally employed by a company in one country but are working full-time for the benefit of a company in another country. 


WPP employees should not negotiate and/or conclude client contracts from a different location to where the associated client revenue will be recognised.  


Furthermore, Group companies should not have a fixed place of business overseas without formally registering as an overseas branch. Failure to follow WPP Policy could result in the creation of a permanent establishment (i.e., a place of business) in the overseas country, resulting in additional taxes becoming due in that country together with various reporting and compliance requirements. 


Please refer to the tax policy in respect of overseas working arrangements (Appendix 3).

## 1.18 Use of freelancers (including consultants and self-employed individuals)


Group companies should have a process in place to determine the applicable taxes that should be deducted on payments to freelancers, including employment taxes and social security (collectively, “payroll taxes”). 


In particular, Group companies should perform a review of the tax status of freelancers when they are initially onboarded, in addition to an annual review of all individuals who are being treated as freelancers, to confirm that their roles have not changed since their joining date.  


Common factors that are used to assess the tax status of freelancers include: 


the length of the contract and time commitment required from the freelancer; 

the level of supervision and control of the freelancer by the company, particularly over how their work is to be delivered and the need to agree absences or working hours; 

the extent to which a freelancer is performing a similar role to that of other employees; 

how the freelancer charges the company for their work, with a daily/hourly rate considered to represent a higher risk of classification as an employee for tax purposes over a fixed-fee arrangement with a set deliverable; 

whether the freelancer incurs costs of running their business which are not recharged; 

the extent to which the freelancer is integrated with the business, for example, attends staff events or is located at the company premises, 

whether the freelancer is personally expected to provide the services, or is able to appoint a substitute worker; and 

if the freelancer is entitled to employee benefits such as holiday pay or sick pay. 


Tax authorities in different countries will focus on the factors above to varying degrees and therefore specific cases will need to be evaluated with reference to country specific guidance. 


Where a freelancer should be taxed in the same way as an employee under applicable legislation, payments should not be made to the freelancer without the deduction of the appropriate payroll taxes. 


For the UK specifically, please refer to section 4 of Appendix 1 for more information about Personal Services Contracts and IR35. 


If you have any questions about the tax requirements of certain individuals, please contact WPP Tax.

## 1.20 Tax reporting


The reported BFC submissions, whether at year end or interims, are expected to reflect all taxes including those accounted for within operating profit.  


Absolute transparency is needed between Group companies and WPP Tax to ensure appropriate accounting and disclosure decisions for external reporting. 


At each reporting event, tax charge and provisioning decisions should reflect the most up to date information to ensure that the Group will have no significant adjustments to the actual tax charge or tax returns.  


In meeting the above, WPP Tax will issue appropriate instructions and timetable, as part of the wider financial reporting instructions, for each reporting event for completion and review of the tax schedules in the Reporting Packs. WPP Group companies should: 


calculate all tax charges in accordance with the Group accounting policy and the appropriate GAAP/IFRS; 

provide timely, complete and accurate tax packs and provision templates in accordance with instructions from WPP Tax; 

share relevant information with WPP Tax; 

assist WPP Tax with questions and clarifications required as part of the reporting process; 

provide separate financial accounts and tax packages to WPP Tax by the end of Q1 for the preceding calendar year-end where multiple legal entities report into one shared BFC Cartesis code (note that multiple legal entities reporting into one shared code is in violation of WPP Policy); 

ensure accurate reporting in year-end schedules in BFC, including year-end schedules and BEAT/IFFRP schedules for US business units; 

notify WPP Tax of new US state or city business registrations or withdrawals, and openings or closures of office locations.** **

## 1.21 Document retention


All jurisdictions have legal requirements that necessitate the retention of financial records or other records that contain tax relevant data. Failure to comply with these requirements can result in significant tax costs to Group companies. 


At a minimum, records, including tax returns, general ledgers and other supporting documentation, should be maintained while the statute of limitations upon which the local tax authorities may assess taxes remains open.  


Statute of limitations for losses and other attributes may not close until several years after the loss or attribute is ultimately used (as opposed to when it is generated) so the ultimate year of close may not be obvious.  


Documentation relating to the tax basis of long-term assets (e.g., fixed assets) should be maintained until the statute of limitation has closed on those tax years in which disposition of these long-term assets occurred.  


Closing binders on the purchase of companies should be saved permanently.  


As regards to computerised records, if a company should change or update the software that they use to maintain their records, they must maintain the ability to run and access the old records while the statute of limitations remains open.  


Tax authorities must be capable of obtaining the record data, which they can use for their auditing purposes. Furthermore, certain countries require financial records to be kept in-country. Due consideration should be made to these requirements in advance of moving any finance processes overseas.

**Freelancers (including Personal Services Companies (“PSCs”)) **


# i) PSCs and IR35


From 6 May 2021 onwards, Group companies will have a legal obligation to determine the IR35 status of all freelancers providing services to WPP via PSCs in the UK. The obligation to perform an IR35 assessment applies regardless of whether the PSC freelancer is provided to WPP via an employment agency or is engaged directly by a Group company. 


All WPP agencies should use the KPMG Off Payroll Management (OPM) tool to conduct IR35 assessments in a consistent way across the Group.  


When assessing freelancers on the KPMG tool, segregation of duties should be in place between those preparing and reviewing the IR35 assessments. Reasonable care must be taken when performing the assessments. 


Status Determination Statements (“SDSs”) that are produced by the KPMG Tool must be shared in a timely manner with the freelancer and their employment agency (if applicable). 


Freelancers are entitled to dispute their SDS. Should they do so, Group companies must respond to the dispute within 45 days.  


Where a freelancer is determined to fall inside IR35 and it is determined that engaging the freelancer via an umbrella company is a suitable option to ensure that employment taxes are deducted as appropriate, Group companies should only accept freelancers who are providing their services to WPP via an FCSA accredited umbrella company

# ii) Sole traders/self-employed freelancers


Where a freelancer is a sole trader, Group companies will be required to perform an employment status determination where the freelancer is directly engaged by the Group company i.e., the Group company has not entered into a contract with an employment agency for the provision of the freelancer’s services. 


Where the above applies, the KPMG Tool can be used to perform the employment status assessment.  


Please note that the Status Determination Statement that is produced by the KPMG Tool does not need to be shared with the freelancer in respect of sole traders. 


If a sole-trader freelancer is engaged via an employment agency, the Group company will not normally be required to perform an employment status assessment. This responsibility will fall to the employment agency. Group companies should, where possible, engage with sole-trader freelancers via an employment agency.

	**Appendix 3: Tax policy for overseas working arrangements ****	 **

# 1. 	Introduction


Employees should work from the country in which they are employed. There are a number of potential legal, tax and employment issues that can arise where employees are not working in the same country as their employing company.  


The purpose of this policy is to set out the measures that WPP Group companies are expected to adopt in order to reduce the risk of creating a Permanent Establishment 

(“PE”) in an overseas country, including as regards to employee hosting arrangements.  


This policy should be read in conjunction with the WPP Internal Mobility Policies which set out the broader issues requiring consideration. Please contact WPP Tax should you have any questions on this policy. 

# 2. 	Policy


In order to reduce the risk of creating a PE in an overseas country:  


there should be no staff negotiating the pricing or key terms of contracts, or concluding contracts (particularly client contracts or large procurement contracts, including media) on behalf of an agency whilst in an overseas country as this creates a Dependent Agent PE; and  


where possible, staff should not work from an overseas country (from home or from the office of another overseas agency) on a permanent or regular basis as this creates a fixed place of business PE. 


In cases where overseas working cannot be avoided on a permanent or regular basis, then hosting arrangements can be considered, subject to review with the network CPO, CFO and WPP. These arrangements need to be structured appropriately from a tax perspective and create additional costs and an administrative burden for the companies concerned. 


**Hosting arrangements:** Where employees are legally employed by a company in one country (the “legal employer”) but are working full-time for the benefit of a company in another country (the “economic employer”): 


a legal agreement should be in place between the legal employer and economic employer for the provision of services by the employee concerned; 

the legal employer should charge the economic employer an arm’s length service fee. How the service fee is calculated will depend on the type of services provided however in the vast majority of cases, it is not sufficient to only recharge salary costs. Attributable overhead costs must be included in cost base of the service fee and in most cases the legal employer should also be entitled to earn a profit margin (e.g., by applying a mark-up to the cost base); 


employees working overseas in this way should not under any circumstance be negotiating the pricing or key terms of contracts, or concluding/signing contracts for their economic employer; and 

where employees perform their employment duties in the same country in which their economic employer is based, as opposed to the country where they are now legally employed, a shadow payroll may need to be operated which is an expensive above the line cost.  

The approach above is only acceptable in respect of isolated cases of hosting.  


Please also note the following: 


Where the economic employer is unable to identify a host company in the overseas country which is part of the same agency, it is preferable, subject to discussion with WPP Tax, for a WPP-branded company to host the employee, rather than a host company belonging to a different agency. Note there are not WPP-branded companies in every jurisdiction. 


Where the recruitment or transfer of an employee overseas is instigated by the economic employer, rather than the employee, and/or there is a commercial advantage for the economic employer to having an employee located in the overseas country, the employer will likely need to formally establish a division of an existing WPP-branded or agency legal entity in the overseas country. This will require a separate BFC reporting unit to be set-up which it will be necessary to manage and fulfil reporting requirements of accordingly. WPP Tax should be consulted on this prior to any decision being made. If this approach is not followed, the employer will likely need to register a PE in the overseas country which is even more burdensome, costly and increases our tax/transfer pricing risk as a Group.  


For further information, please refer to the full policy and guidance on remote working arrangements, which is available on the tax page on InsideWPP.
