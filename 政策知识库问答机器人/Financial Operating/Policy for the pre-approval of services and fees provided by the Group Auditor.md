**1. Annual pre-approval policy and procedures **


**Statement of principle regarding all services **

The audit committee is required to pre-approve all audit and non-audit services provided by the independent auditor.  Pre-approval is required to ensure that the non-audit services do not impair the auditor’s independence. Services that have not received general pre-approval will require specific review and pre-approval by the audit committee as outlined in this policy, unless they are individually lower than £25k, in which case the Group Financial Controller is authorised to administer this pre-approval policy, subject to final approval by the Audit Committee at the next Audit Committee meeting.   

Appendices A and B describe the audit and audit-related services that are pre-approved by the audit committee. The term of any pre-approval is for the calendar year following the date of authorisation unless the audit committee specifically notes otherwise. The audit committee will periodically review the list of pre-approved services.  

Notwithstanding the above, if any services pre-approved in or by this this policy become prohibited for the auditor to provide as a result of changes to relevant independence rules, those services will cease to be approved and will not be provided by the auditor.  

**Purpose of pre-approval procedures  **

The policies and procedures herein ensure compliance with SEC and FRC regulations by providing the audit committee with a comprehensive list of services for which the company may engage its independent auditor and to facilitate efficient administration of the reporting and approval process. 

All proposed engagements not covered by this policy require pre-approval by the chair of the audit committee as outlined below. 

**Revised FRC Ethical Standard – January 2024 **

The Revised FRC Ethical Standard – January 2024, becomes effective from 15 December 

2024. Services that fall into the categories of 5.40 of the FRC Ethical Standard (known as ‘the whitelist’ and included in Appendix C), which are considered permissible by the FRC have been updated in the Revised FRC Ethical Standard. These may be provided by the auditor subject to this pre-approval policy. These restrictions apply to WPP plc and its worldwide subsidiaries.  

In addition, no services are permitted under a contingent fee arrangement, on a loan staff/secondment arrangement, or that contain elements that may result in the auditor undertaking a management or employee function or role. 

**Cap on non-audit services **

In authorising work and spend for permitted services, the audit committee must consider the impact of the proposed work and fees on the actual and perceived independence of the auditors; and also consider whether the auditors represent the most effective and efficient provider of the service. 

The FRC Ethical Standard maintains a financial cap on non-audit services performed by the auditors. The cap comprises two calculations, as below:  

4.13 	**When the audit firm, or a member of its network, provides to a public interest entity that it audits, its parent undertaking or its controlled undertakings, non****-****audit services other than those referred to in Regulation 80 of The Statutory Auditors and Third Country Auditors (Amendment) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019 (SI 2019/177): **

**the total fees for such services provided to the audited entity and its controlled undertakings shall be limited to no more than 70% of the average of the fees paid in the last three consecutive financial years for the audit(s) of the audited entity and of its controlled undertakings and of the consolidated financial statements of that group of undertakings; and **

**the total fees for such services provided by the audit firm shall be limited to no more than 70% of the average of the fees paid to the audit firm in the last three consecutive financial years for the audit(s) of the audited entity and, where applicable, of its parent undertaking, of its controlled undertakings and of the consolidated financial statements of that group of undertakings. **

The audit fees are for the statutory audit only, including the consolidation and fees for subsidiary audits, but excluding additional work such as the 20-F and SOX related work. 

**Annual pre-approval process **

The audit committee will pre-approve the services in Appendix A and B and this policy once a year. 


**Role of Group Financial Controller  **


The audit committee cannot delegate its responsibility to pre-approve services to management. The audit committee may delegate pre-approval authority to one or more of its members, who will report decisions at the next scheduled meeting or an agreed time. 

However, the chair of the audit committee has, on behalf of the audit committee, agreed that the Group Financial Controller is authorised to administer this pre-approval policy on behalf of the audit committee. Administering will include summarising approved spend and agreeing that summary with the auditor for the audit committee at the half-year and yearend, or on request, or if spend approaches the overriding cap as a percentage of the audit fees as outlined above. The auditor will have the responsibility for outlining and discussing such spend with the audit committee. 


**Pre-approved and permitted services **


**Audit Services and fees **

In respect of the audit of WPP plc and those subsidiaries required to be audited for group scope or due to local legislation, whilst formal appointment of the group auditor is ratified at the AGM each year, audit services provided by the current appointed group auditor are preapproved per Appendix A. 

The audit committee also pre-approves the other audit services that the independent auditor can reasonably provide, as listed in Appendix A. 

**Non-audit services and fees **


**WPP Policy **

Non-audit fees will not exceed 50% of total audit fees (including audit-related services), unless specifically agreed to by more than 50% of the audit committee including the chairperson. 

**Audit-related services **

Audit-related services are those non-audit services that are: largely carried out by members of the audit engagement team; where the work involved is closely related to work performed in the audit; where the threats to auditor independence are clearly insignificant; and as a consequence safeguards need not be applied.  

Such services include: 

Reporting required by law or regulation to be provided by the auditor; 

Reviews of interim financial information; 

Reporting on regulatory returns; 

Reporting to a regulator on client assets; 

Reporting on government grants; 

Reporting on internal financial controls when required by law or regulation; 

Extended audit work that is authorised by those charged with governance performed on financial information and/or financial controls where this work is integrated with the audit work and is performed on the same principal terms and conditions. The audit committee has determined that the audit-related services listed in Appendix B do not impair the independence of the auditor. These pre-approved services are subject to a maximum individual fee of £250k. Audit-related services in excess of this fee or not listed in Appendix B must be pre-approved separately by the audit committee or audit committee chair as delegate. 



**Earn-out and related work **

Earn-out work has been preapproved on the basis it meets the criteria under the whitelist of services that are subject to the fee cap, being “work or agreed upon procedures, authorised by those charged with governance performed on material included within or referenced from the annual report of an entity relevant to an engagement.” Each financial year, pre-approval of authorised spend will be confirmed by the Audit Committee with reference to planned earn-out related services to be performed by the group auditor.  The chair of the committee will be required to authorise spend exceeding the pre-approved amount as outlined below.   

**Other services **

The committee may pre-approve permissible non-audit services classified as “other services” if the services would not impair the independence of the auditor and fit into the categories included in the whitelist (Appendix C). The services, which have been preapproved by the audit committee, are listed below. These pre-approved services are subject to a maximum individual fee of £250k. Services in excess of this fee or that are not listed below must be pre-approved separately by the audit committee. 

The pre-approved services include the provision of:  

educational and informational programs, seminars, and related services, such as live programs, webcasts (including webcast series), podcasts, websites, database subscriptions (including some that provide access to the independent auditor’s proprietary information and tools that offer technical support and advice), checklists, research reports, surveys, published books and other materials, applications, local office seminars, Technical Library and conferences. 

Other agreed upon procedures services that do not meet the FRC definition of audit-related, but are permissible under the whitelist in Appendix C: “Additional assurance work or agreed upon procedures, authorised by those charged with governance performed on material included within or referenced from the annual report of an entity relevant to an engagement.”. This definition includes client audits.  


**Other services requiring ad hoc approval and process **

If services that are not covered by the pre-approval policy come to the attention of the Group Financial Controller, they will require approval by the audit committee. The audit committee has delegated the pre-approval authority to the audit committee chair, who may approve any additional services. Group Financial Controller will summarise these services, and agree that summary with the auditor, for the next audit committee meeting, whereby the auditor will outline and discuss the services with the audit committee. 


**WPP Group works performed for PwC **

Any work (e.g. media, creative, advertising) that WPP group entities1 are performing for any PwC entities must be communicated to and pre-approved by the Group Financial Controller. The nature of any work provided to PwC must be a simple business relationship, not a joint relationship (i.e. PwC and WPP brands cannot ‘go to market’ together), priced on an arms-length basis and the value of services must be immaterial relative to the total revenue of the individual specific WPP legal entity providing the services to PwC. 

1 WPP group entities includes all WPP HQ, Networks and Agency entities. 


**6. Policy on hiring from WPP’s retained auditor **


WPP head office as well as all networks and agencies should not engage in any recruitment conversations with any individual from WPP’s retained audit firm, PwC (note, this policy applies to any individual currently employed by PwC and any individual previously employed by PwC in the last 12 months), even if that individual has applied directly for the role via a public advert. If there is an exceptional circumstance where this general policy needs to be varied, then WPP’s Group Financial Controller must be consulted, and approval sought before any conversations or interviews occur.
