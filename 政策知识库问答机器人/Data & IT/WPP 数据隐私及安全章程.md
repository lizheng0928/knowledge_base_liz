Section 2: WPP DATA CODE OF CONDUCT
2 WPP DATA CODE OF CONDUCT
2.1 Our Principles
WPP, its companies and its people are committed to responsible collection, management, use and
protection of data.
WPP recognises its obligations to all its stakeholders including share owners, clients, its own people,
suppliers and consumers.
WPP works with many categories of data and uses the term ‘data’ in its broadest sense. We include
within this definition client data, consumer data and all information and data related to the operation of
our businesses.
2.2 Our Practices
We will be transparent with consumers.
We will treat data in accordance with all applicable laws, regulations and treaties.
We will implement fair and reasonable data policies and procedures.
We will treat data as confidential.
We will understand not only what data we hold but also its relevance to stakeholders.
We will secure, collect, process, use and store data appropriately.
We will ensure that data is retained appropriately.
We will implement necessary and appropriate technical measures to secure data.
We will delete data when required to do so.
We will ensure our people understand their role in upholding these principles and practices.

Section 3: WPP DATA ETHICS POLICY
3 WPP DATA ETHICS POLICY
3.1 Introduction
The WPP Data Ethics Policy applies to all Operating Units wherever they are located in the world and
regardless of regional variations in law or culture. Operating Units should designate a person to take
responsibility for compliance with this Policy.
All WPP staff are expected to read and adhere to the Policy as it applies to their job function.
3.2 WPP Data Ethics Statement
Ethical data use at WPP is everyone’s responsibility.
Ethics-by-design drives our data use.
Data ethics evaluation is built into every stage of our data use.
We recognise that there is no ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach to data ethics and we will consider the
benefits of processing against any impact on individuals, the wider community and the environment
as well as our people, clients, and investor partners.
We acknowledge our responsibility to understand both the limitations and possibilities of data and to
ensure that any processing is proportionate to our needs.
We encourage our people to challenge data use and speak up if they have a concern.
We will be transparent regarding our ethical decision-making process and will educate our people on
ethical data use.
3.3 Applying Data Ethics
3.3.1 Working with Clients, Partners and Suppliers
Operating Units should only work with those clients, partners and suppliers who share our data and
ethical values.
Clients, partners and suppliers determine their ethical appetite, Operating Units should maintain the
‘chain of trust’ in these relationships.
3.3.2 Initial assessments and identifying risks
Operating Units are required to ensure that any data processing must have a clear and well
understood purpose. Operating Units must not collect and process data on a ‘just in case’ basis.
All proposed data processing must be assessed by the Operating Unit before implementation or use
to identify ‘red flags’ and/or high-risk processing from a data ethics point of view. Operating Units
must:
 understand and document the data being processed and any ethical concerns and decisions;
 respect the right of individuals to privacy and dignity and avoid both discrimination and loss of
choice and control because of the use of data; and
 involve stakeholders to ensure diversity of thought and experience in assessing ethical use of
data.
Data Processing must not start or, if already underway, then stopped if the Operating Unit determines that
the processing does not meet our ethical standards.

3.3.3 Accountability
Operating Units must:
 be transparent regarding the ethical decision-making process;
 educate staff and teams on ethical data use;
 implement procedures for design, testing, validation, documentation, data management, and
record keeping; and
 implement processes to enable staff to challenge data use and question whether the

4 WPP ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE POLICY
4.1 Introduction
With the prevalence and availability of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Operating Units may be seeking to
implement or have already implemented AI in various functions of its business including services and
deliverables provided to clients but also internal processes such as recruitment, human resources,
and finance functions such as cost control.
The WPP Artificial Intelligence Policy applies to all Operating Units wherever they are located in the
world and regardless of regional variations in law or culture. Operating Units should designate a
person to take responsibility for compliance with this Policy.
All WPP staff are expected to read and adhere to the Policy as it applies to their job function. In
addition, the WPP AI Governance Framework applies to all uses and applications of AI and
Generative AI.
4.2 WPP Artificial Intelligence Principles
The following AI Principles apply to all uses and applications of AI in WPP Operating Units:
1. Understanding AI’s potential and limits
We acknowledge our responsibility to understand both the limitations and possibilities of AI.
We remain objective in assessing its impact, recognising both its potential benefits and risks.
We are committed to educating our people about the opportunities and challenges AI
presents, both directly and indirectly.
2. AI as augmentation, not replacement
AI supports and complements our creativity and capabilities; it is not a substitute for human
judgment and expertise.
We use AI to enhance and complement our work, helping us deliver better outcomes for our
people, clients, and stakeholders.
We prioritise human oversight at all stages of AI development, deployment and use.
3. Data provenance, quality, and bias
We understand the provenance, quality, and potential biases of the data used to train our
chosen AI systems.
We only use AI where we are confident in the data sources and have the appropriate rights to
use the results and outputs generated.
We consider the ethical and sustainability implications of the data and systems used.
4. Transparency, explainability and responsible use
We are transparent with our clients, our people, and the wider community about how we use
AI.
We explain how AI is used in our services, making clear where our work includes AI-driven
outputs.
Our use of AI complies with applicable regulations and WPP policies, including those related
to data ethics, artificial intelligence, and data privacy and security.

4. We are transparent to our clients, our people and the wider community about how we
use generative AI.
We explain to clients if Generative AI is used in our services to them, making clear where our

4.5 AI Security Risk Management
The development and deployment of AI, and AI-enabled systems, whether for internal or client-facing
purposes, create cybersecurity and business continuity risks. Operating Units must assess and
monitor these risks at all stages of AI development, procurement, deployment, and cessation.
Operating Units shall address each of the following areas in their security plans when developing,
procuring, or using AI systems, and shall document the same. These measures supplement, and do
not replace, WPP’s minimum security standards outlined in the WPP SECURITY & TECHNOLOGY
POLICY.
4.5.1 Security-by-Design
Operating Units must adopt security-by-design principles when developing or integrating AI systems.
Specifically, at inception, those wishing to develop and/or deploy an AI system should consult WPP
Digital Security regarding the development, integration, and deployment of the tool to identify and
mitigate security-related risks, as appropriate.
4.5.2 Access Controls for AI Systems and Training Data
AI models and associated data sets, including training data, must be subject to heightened access
controls, including:
 Role-based access controls (RBAC) or attribute-based access controls (ABAC) where
feasible to limit access to only necessary personnel, distinguishing between users and
administrators;
 Separate administrator accounts, distinct from the administrators’ day-to-day
user/administrative account(s); and
 Multifactor authentication (MFA) and privileged-access workstations (PAWs) for
administrative access. A credential-management system may be used to limit, manage, and
monitor credential use.
4.5.3 Penetration Tests and Vulnerability Assessments
In collaboration with WPP Digital Security, Operating Units must conduct periodic vulnerability
assessments (and, where appropriate, penetration testing) on AI systems prior to deployment and
thereafter at a frequency appropriate to the use case of the AI system, but no less frequent than
annually.
4.5.4 Audit Trail
Operating Units must ensure there are audit trails that enable the detection of, and response to,
material cybersecurity events, operational disruption, or unexpected outputs. The nature of this audit
trail will vary between AI systems but may include periodic snapshots of the underlying model, training
data, and outputs. Audit teams should ensure that reasonable monitoring, controls, and reporting are
in place to manage the context-specific risks posed by AI.

4.5.5 Continuous Validation of AI System Integrity
Operating Units must validate AI systems before and during use to ensure they have not been
tampered with. Validation methods may include creation of hashes and encrypted copies of each
release of an AI model or system, storage of all forms of code in a version-control system with
appropriate access controls, evaluating and securing a supply chain for any external AI models and
data, and carefully inspecting models inside a secure development zone prior to deployment. Logging
of AI systems (including login IPs, accounts associated with malicious queries, etc.) should feed into
existing security event management and incident response processes with tailored automated alerts
and triggers.
4.5.6 Document AI System Supply Chains
Operating Units must document the development and deployment lifecycle of AI systems, including
both hardware and software components. This may include documenting the software supply chain
through software bills of materials (SBOMs), as well as security-relevant information like sources of
training data, intended scope and limitations, guardrails, cryptographic hashes or signatures, retention
time, suggested review frequency, and potential failure modes.
4.5.7 Third Party AI System Diligence
When integrating AI systems from third-party service providers, Operating Units must ensure that they
have conducted cybersecurity and privacy-related diligence in accordance with relevant WPP
policies/procedures to include, inter alia: (i) IT security controls, including with regard to connectivity
between WPP and the third-party service provider’s systems; (ii) model validation and maintenance;
(iii) data retention/deletion; and (iv) onward data sharing and/or collateral use of WPP or WPP
customer data.
4.5.8 Cloud Security Measures
Operating Units must ensure that appropriate cloud security measures are in place, where applicable.
For example, as appropriate: (i) secure cloud key management processes; (ii) network segmentation;
(iii) encryption in the cloud; (iv) ensuring appropriate access to, and integration of, cloud logs to
enable threat hunting operations, including object versioning; (v) configuring data retention policies;
and (vi) preventing access over public IPs.
4.5.9 Assess Operational Resilience
When developing or deploying an AI system, Operating Units should ensure operational resilience
through, inter alia, model and system backups maintained in accordance with WPP’s standard
security requirements, and failover systems to ensure continued operation. Key performance
indicators of system availability and performance should be reviewed periodically.
4.5.10 Secure Deletion Capabilities
Operating Units must implement secure deletion capabilities that perform autonomous and
irretrievable deletion of components, such as training and validation models or cryptographic keys, in
accordance with the WPP DATA HANDLING AND RETENTION POLICY.

5 WPP PRIVACY POLICY
Our Data Code of Conduct states:
“WPP, its companies and its people are committed to responsible collection, management, use and

How you use data and personal information not only reflects on you as an individual but also on us as
an organisation. This policy applies to all full and part time employees and contractors of WPP
who handle data and personal information or use WPP’s information systems.
See section 15 WPP FAIR PROCESSING NOTICE for information on how the personal information of
those working for WPP Operating Units is processed.
5.1 What is Personal information?
Personal information is any information or an opinion relating to an identifiable person
including name, address, telephone number, email address (including work email address), national
identification number or other such identifiers. The sources of personal information vary considerably
and include:
 email and attachments;
 databases and/or online systems containing personal information, social media or other
mass communication tools, etc.
 websites - online employee directories, online survey data collection, etc.;
 CCTV and physical access to sites - data stored on electronic key cards, location
tracking, etc.;
 paper documents – employee and client contracts, letters, memos, reports, etc; and
 photographs – office badges, security passes, employee records, etc.
The definition of personal information varies significantly based on country-specific data
protection laws. For example, under data protection laws in many countries the term “personal data”
now includes online identifiers, such as cookie IDs, social media handles, device fingerprints and
other IDs used for online tracking whilst in South Africa, “personal information” includes information
pertaining to juristic persons, such as companies. Also, be aware that some client contracts may
include a definition of personal information relating to that client that is broader than the legal
definition. WPP Operating Units must work closely with clients to ensure that personal information
is handled and protected in a way that is consistent with the contract.
5.2 What is Data Privacy?
Data Privacy is:
 protection of personal information – restricted access and security of data;
 the expectation that personal information collected, used and shared with others will be
obtained, protected, used and disclosed in a way that the local and regional laws allow;
 being transparent with individuals about what data is being collected about them, how that
data will be used and who it will be shared with;
 limiting the personal information collected, and the period of time for which it is stored in
identifiable form;
 keeping personal information accurate and up-to-date;

information without a lawful basis – we should always make sure that we are relying on the

implement the required procedures. Applicable Laws must be considered in contract reviews and

5.5 Security
Wherever they are located in the world, WPP Operating Units must have in place reasonable,
appropriate controls to prevent unauthorised access to WPP data, sites and systems.

smartphones or mobile phones, tablets, and memory sticks;

finance files, client files and physical site access; and

personal information across national borders.

manner, the Operating Unit will itself be directly liable for the underlying breach of the Applicable
Laws. If you have any questions regarding data privacy and security, please contact dpo@wpp.com.

6 WPP SECURITY & TECHNOLOGY POLICY
6.1 Introduction
WPP is a data driven business and it is critical that it ensures that data is always used and protected
appropriately.
The purpose of this Security & Technology Policy (“Policy”) is to set out best practice principles in
relation to Security and Technology operations for WPP’s Operating Units (“Operating Units”).
The Policy applies to all Operating Units wherever they are located in the world and regardless of how
their IT service is provided (either internally or externally). Operating Units should designate a person
to take responsibility for compliance with this Policy.
All WPP staff are expected to read and adhere to the Policy as it applies to their job function.
This policy is designed to create and set a minimal viable operating environment to protect WPP, our
people and our clients. Where a business operates with a higher level of risk, including but not limited
to working with high-profile or politically exposed clients or handling sensitive data or Intellectual
Property (both client and our own), a risk assessment must be performed and additional security
controls should be put in place to mitigate any identified heightened risk.
6.2 Physical Security
Physical security is at the heart of any organisation’s successful security strategy. Operating Units
wherever they are located in the world must have in place reasonable and appropriate controls to
prevent unauthorised access to Operating Unit systems and to prevent loss of data.
Whilst WPP recognises that the physical and environmental security challenges will vary between
Operating Units, the controls outlined below are recognised as good security protocols to have in
place and Operating Units should develop and document physical and environmental security
processes and procedures that incorporate controls such as those identified in this Policy.
6.2.1 Physical Security Plan
Operating Units must have a plan for physical security that is reviewed and updated (on no less than
an annual basis) by an individual nominated by the Operating Unit.
6.2.2 Physical Security Access Controls
Operating Units must operate an access control system to its site(s) e.g. swipe cards, combination
door locks, lock & key, and video recording to ensure that only authorised persons are able to access

11 WPP BUSINESS CONTINUITY POLICY
11.1 Introduction
WPP has a responsibility to maintain delivery of critical business services and processes for our
clients and employees.
During a disruptive incident, business operations may be at risk of being temporarily interrupted. A
business continuity plan documents the procedures that guide the Company during and immediately
after an incident with the aim of minimising impact to revenue, reputation, employee safety,
operations and legal and regulatory compliance through improving preparedness to maintain
continuity of priority processes.
This policy sets out the minimum standards for compliance to the Business Continuity Policy.
11.2 Definitions
In this Policy the following terms will have the following meanings:
a. Business Impact Analysis (BIA) is the process by which the Company establishes what are its
most urgent services and processes to maintain or recover as a result of disruption, the timeline in
which their loss will have a critical impact on the Company, what workarounds and resources are
required to deliver the process during disruption and what are the supporting dependencies for
delivery.
b. Business Continuity Advocate means the person assigned to complete the BIA and BCP.
c. Business Continuity Plan (BCP) means documented that includes the action plans the
Company should undertake to maintain delivery of priority business processes to a pre-defined
level of operation following the disruption as set out in paragraph 11.4.
d. Business Continuity Plan Test means the process to review and test the relevance and
accuracy of content in the BCP.
e. Certification means the document awarded to the Company upon completion of the annual BCP
Test once the BCP Test report has been validated.
f. Company (the Company) means WPP entity, agency network, department or function
responsible for maintaining operations at the location where they are based.
g. Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) means the documented process or set of procedures to recover
and protect IT infrastructure and systems that priority processes are dependent upon for delivery.
h. Designated Authority means the Company executive officer responsible for approving the
content of the BIA and BCP.
i. Global Resilience Lead (GRL) means the person designated by the company as responsible for
ensuring the Company is compliant with WPP Policy.
j. Incident means a situation that leads to a business disruption, impairment, interruption, loss,
emergency or crisis adversely impacting Company's ability to provide any aspect of Critical
Services.
k. Priority Processes means those the BIA has calculated as the most urgent processes to
maintain or recover during and immediately after a disruption. This will include those the
Company is legally bound to provide to its employees.
l. Recovery Time Objective (RTO) means the time by which the process needs to be available to
a minimum level or risk having a critical and negative impact on Company finances, operations,
reputation or violate legal and regulatory requirements.

13.3 What is a Data Security Event?
Security Event means any occurrence that might breach information security policy, controls or any
other event that could lead to a Security Incident. A Data Security Incident is an event or series of
events that have a significant probability of compromising the confidentiality, integrity or availability of
WPP Data. This would include the unauthorized access to, unauthorized acquisition of, disclosure or
use of, or loss of WPP Data.
Examples of Data Security Incident include (but are not limited to):
 A WPP user is tricked into opening an attachment sent via email that is malicious.
 The theft or physical loss of IT assets including laptops, storage devices (such as USB

 The theft or physical loss of paper records containing WPP Data such as loss or theft of a
bag or file containing papers with WPP Data.
 High volumes of connection requests sent to a web server, causing it to crash or fail to
respond. (Denial of Service).
 A server known to hold WPP Data which is accessed or otherwise compromised by an
unauthorized party.
 Improper disposal of records, media or equipment containing WPP Data.
 Accidental or intentional transmission of WPP Data to the wrong person, such as an
unencrypted file containing individuals’ Personal Data that is emailed to the wrong recipient.
 A WPP User provides or exposes sensitive information or WPP Data to others through peer-
to-peer file sharing services.
 An externally created deepfake video or audio recording is used to impersonate a WPP
executive or client representative, resulting in significant reputational damage, financial loss
due to fraudulent transactions, or legal liabilities.
 A critical AI model is maliciously corrupted, leading to the generation of offensive,
discriminatory, or problematic content.

14.2 What are Data Subjects’ Rights under GDPR?
Data subjects have a number of rights under GDPR. The key rights are set out in Chapter III of the
GDPR, and are:
 Right of access (Article 15)
 Right to rectification (Article 16)
 Right to erasure (the ‘right to be forgotten’) (Article 17)
 Right to restriction of processing (Article 18)
 Right to data portability (Article 20)
 Right to object (Article 21)
 Rights in relation to automated decision-making (Article 22)
Some of these rights (such as the right to data portability) are new under GDPR. Other rights already
existed in EU data protection law but are expanded under GDPR.
In this document, we use the term “Data Subject Request” to refer to a request by a data subject to
exercise any of these rights.
Responsibility for complying with these rights rests with the controller. This means that if a WPP
company holds personal data as a processor on behalf of a client, it is not obliged to respond to a
Data Subject Request (and it may be a breach of the company’s contract with the client for the
company to respond directly). If a WPP company receives a Data Subject Request in relation to
personal data in respect of which the company is a processor, it should forward the request to the
client. Companies should be aware that the contract may specify a timeframe within which the
request must be forwarded to the client, and how the request should be forwarded.

14.8 Data relating to other people

provide that information to the data subject. If it is possible to anonymise the information by removing

data subject. However, the individual’s identity may be obvious (even if only to the data subject
making the request) even with these identifiers removed, and in these cases the information should
not be provided at all.

unreasonable to provide it, a WPP company must not provide that information in response to a
request – to do so is likely to contravene the company’s obligations under GDPR.

merely seeking to harass the WPP company with numerous requests. It cannot be relied upon
merely because a single request is expressed in broad terms (a data subject is entitled to ask a
controller to provide a copy of all personal data held by the controller), or because it appears that the
data subject’s motive is to obtain information that may be helpful in actual or potential litigation.
14.10 Member State Exemptions
GDPR permits Member States to add further exemptions in their national law. For example, the UK
GDPR exempts various kinds of information from the right of access, including information subject to
legal professional privilege, confidential employment references and information about the company’s
intentions in negotiations with the data subject (if providing that information would prejudice those
negotiations).
Note: These exemptions vary between Member States, and so for any non-routine requests it is
important to check whether any exemptions apply in national law before responding to a Data Subject
Request.
14.11 Verifying the Data Subject’s Identity
It is critical that a WPP company verifies the identity of the individual making a Data Subject Request.
This is important for all Data Subject Requests, but particularly so with subject access and portability,
which could, without the proper identity checks, result in personal data being disclosed to the wrong
person.
WPP companies need to strike the right balance between properly verifying the individual’s identity
and appearing unreasonable or obstructive. For example, if a current or former employee makes a
request using a postal or email address that the company knows to be the employee’s, it would not be
reasonable to request a proof of address from the individual. However, when there is any doubt
about the identity of the person making a request, or if the person making a request uses details that
the company does not recognise (for example, a new address), the company should request a copy
of an identity document bearing the data subject’s signature and address (such as a driver’s licence).
Requests are sometimes made by third parties on behalf of a data subject – such as a firm of
solicitors. Requests from solicitors are likely to indicate that information is being sought in connection
with legal proceedings and should be referred to WPP Legal. In any case, the WPP company should
request a signed authorisation from the data subject, together with a copy of an identity document
bearing the data subject’s signature and address (such as a driver’s licence).

14.12 Dealing with Requests by Online IDs
More complicated issues may arise where an individual makes a request by reference to an online
identifier (such as a cookie ID that the individual has found in his/her browser’s storage). GDPR
makes clear that personal data includes information about an individual who can be identified using
an “online identifier”, so information associated with a unique cookie ID is in principle personal data.
However, it may be difficult or impossible for a WPP company to verify that the person making a
request is in fact the person to whom the cookie ID (and any associated data) relates, because the
company will probably not recognise the other identifying information supplied by that person (such as
a name or email address). This means that there is a risk that the WPP company will inadvertently
disclose information relating to another person.
WPP companies need to take a fact-sensitive approach, taking into account both the level of
confidence that the request is genuine and any potential harm that might result from wrongful
disclosure. In general, if the company is satisfied that it has verified the identity of the individual
making the request, it would be reasonable to provide general information associated with the
identifier (e.g. broad demographic segments to which the identifier has been allocated), but not highly
detailed information (such as details of specific websites visited).
WPP companies should not automatically refuse to respond to requests made by reference to an
online identifier, if they are satisfied about the identity of the person making the request. This is likely
to be a contravention of the individual’s rights under GDPR. Companies should also be mindful of the
possibility of a regulatory complaint if they unreasonably refuse to comply with a request. However,
the threat of a complaint should never result in a company releasing information if it is not satisfied
about the identity of the person making the request.
In all cases when dealing with a request made by reference to an online identifier, the WPP company
should ensure that the person making the request has completed a declaration in the form set out in
section 14.17 ‘Form of Data Subject Request Declaration’. Whilst a company cannot normally require
a Data Subject Request to be made in a particular form, a company is entitled to demand reasonable
evidence as to the identity of the person making the request.
14.13 How long do we have to respond?
The normal requirement is to respond to a Data Subject Request without undue delay and within one
month from the date of receipt of the request. (This is shorter than the period provided in some EU
Member States’ national laws prior to GDPR.) Don’t forget the requirement to respond “without undue
delay” – supervisory authorities will very rarely take action if a response is sent within the one-month
deadline, but WPP companies should aim to respond promptly rather than on the last day of the one-
month period.
For complex or large-scale requests the period can be extended by up to two further months. This
should be reserved for exceptional cases only, and not relied on routinely. WPP companies should
expect to be challenged by supervisory authorities where they have extended the time period and be
prepared to justify the extension. Companies must also inform the data subject within the initial one-
month deadline if they intend to extend the period for a response and must explain the reasons for the
extension.
14.14 Can we charge a fee?

responding to a data subject access request. Under GDPR, a WPP company cannot normally charge
a fee for complying with a Data Subject Request.

codes of practice.
 Chief Information Security Officer (CISO): The CISO is responsible for overseeing the digital
security risk management program globally. This includes ensuring that risks are identified,
mitigated, and monitored across all regions. The CISO also communicates significant digital
security risks to senior leadership, risk committee and the WPP Board.

effectively.

The WPP Risk Management Framework (RMF) provides a systematic process for managing digital
security risks. It ensures that risks are proactively addressed and consistently managed across WPP.

16.3.1 Risk Definition
A risk is a potential future event or condition that, if it occurs, may have a positive or negative impact
on objectives. Risks are uncertain; they have not yet occurred but could happen.
An issue is a problem or situation that has already occurred and needs immediate resolution. Issues
are certain; they are present realities.
16.3.2 Risk Appetite and Risk Tolerance
The first step in the RMF is setting out and defining both risk appetite and risk tolerance. WPP defines
and understands these terms as follows:
 Risk Appetite: Understanding and documenting the amount of risk WPP is willing to allow or
accept in the pursuit of business objectives.
 Risk Tolerance: Understanding and documenting the acceptable level of variation from risk
appetite that WPP is willing to allow or accept in pursuit of business objectives.
16.3.3 Risk Identification
The second step in the RMF is identifying and articulating potential risks. Risk identification is the
proactive discovery of risk sources for e.g. vulnerabilities, threats, or weaknesses, as well as risk
events, their causes and their potential consequences and impacts against WPP’s digital security
assets.

16.3.5 Risk Mitigation and Control
Once risks have been assessed and prioritized, the next step is to determine how they should be
treated. Risk mitigation involves implementing actions to reduce the likelihood and/or impact of risks.
Depending on the level of risk, one of the following approaches must be taken:
 Risk Mitigation: Where the risk is above WPP’s risk appetite, the implementation of controls to
minimize the risk’s likelihood and/or impact takes place (e.g. improving processes, enhancing
security measures).
 Risk Transfer: Transfer the risk to a third party, such as insurance or outsourcing specific
operations. Please note that only the responsibility of the risk is transferred and not the overall
accountability, which may still reside with WPP.
 Risk Acceptance: Accept the risk for an accepted and agreed period of time if the cost of
mitigating it outweighs the potential damage and/or the risk is already below WPP risk appetite.

16.3.7 Risk Monitoring
Risks are not static, they evolve over time due to changing environments, technologies, or business
practices. Risk monitoring involves continuously observing both the risks themselves and the
effectiveness of the controls put in place. This ensures that new risks are identified promptly and that
previously mitigated risks do not re-emerge.
There are two key methods by which effective risk monitoring in WPP occurs:
 Continuous Monitoring: tools and processes to continuously monitor risks. This includes
automated vulnerability scans, security audits, and manual assessments.
 Key Risk Indicators (KRIs): Specific metrics developed to monitor critical risks.
The CISO is responsible for establishing and overseeing a centralised Risk Register that captures all
Digital Security risks identified at a global, network, Operating Unit, agency and department level. The
centralised Risk Register must roll up to the Enterprise Risk Analytics Platform.
All identified Digital Security risks and exceptions must be documented in the centralised Risk
Register which will serve as the authoritative source for tracking and managing Digital Security risks
across WPP.
16.3.8 Risk Reporting and Communication
Risk reporting ensures that key stakeholders, including senior leadership and the WPP Board, are
aware of the current WPP risk landscape. Effective communication of risk allows for informed
decision-making and ensures accountability.
From a formality perspective, the following arrangements described below must be followed:
 Internal Reporting: Each Business Security Services lead submits a monthly risk report to the
CISO, detailing newly identified risks, updates on previously identified risks, and the progress of
mitigation plans. This information is consolidated into a global report, which is reviewed quarterly
by the CISO, Audit Committee and WPP Board when required.
 Critical Risk Escalation: If a risk is classified as critical (critical likelihood and critical impact), it
must be immediately escalated to the CISO. A detailed treatment action plan must be developed
within 48 hours to address the risk.

16.3.9 Continuous Improvement
WPP is committed to continuously improving its risk management practices. This involves regular
reviews and updates to this policy based on evolving risks, regulatory changes, or lessons learned
from past incidents. Post-incident reviews must be conducted after any major security event to identify
what worked well and what didn’t, ensuring that similar incidents are handled more effectively in the
future.
