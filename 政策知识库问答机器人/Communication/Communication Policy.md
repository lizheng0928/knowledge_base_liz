# Overview

For the purposes of this policy, “communications” refers to the dissemination of corporate information and content to our own people and externally to the media, share owners, existing and potential clients, regulators, government and nongovernmental organisations, and the public at large. 

Each agency is responsible for establishing its own internal and external communications policies and practices. However, consideration should always be given to the connection between agencies and the wider Group, and to the potential impact of communications within agencies on WPP. 

# Media relations, announcements and approvals

Media enquiries relating to WPP, including requests for comment or interviews, should be referred to the WPP Communications team. Only certain members of the WPP Communications team and WPP senior management team are authorised to speak to the media on behalf of WPP. 

The WPP Communications team is responsible for the preparation, approval and dissemination of WPP press releases, and works with other functions such as Company Secretariat, Finance and Investor Relations on Stock Exchange announcements including financial results announcements. 

The Communications team is also responsible for internal WPP announcements and messages to all staff, leadership groups and other internal audiences. 

Each agency is responsible for its own communications with journalists, other external audiences and its own people on day-to-day matters. However, whenever there is to be an announcement, press release, statement, comment or other forms of external or internal communication involving or mentioning WPP, or that might impact on WPP in any way, they must be discussed and cleared *before* *release* with the WPP Communications team.  

This includes but is not limited to news of top-level appointments, major client wins/losses and news involving more than one WPP company or a WPP client team, acquisitions, strategic alliances, joint ventures, any information concerning the current or future financial performance of a company, sensitive client or employee issues, legal cases, disciplinary matters, closures, senior departures, redundancies, crises/emergencies and anything else that may cause a reputational risk. If in doubt, alert and consult the WPP Communications team, who will provide advice and handle the approval process with WPP senior management as required. 

WPP Country Managers should agree a process for handling and approving communications activity in specific markets with the central WPP Communications team.  

# Corporate identity

The use of the WPP name, logo and descriptors such as boilerplates must adhere to the WPP Brand Guidelines, which are available from the WPP Communications team. 

Any proposed new external or internal brand identity that uses the WPP name or logo must be developed in consultation with and approved via the WPP Communications team. 

No WPP-branded websites, social media accounts, apps, newsletters or other digital 

channels and profiles can be created without approval from the WPP Communications team. 

Campus branding must follow the WPP Campus Brand Guidelines, which are available from the WPP Communications team. The use of the WPP logo and brand identity in Campuses must be approved via the WPP Communications team.

Any event or issue with the potential to damage the reputation of WPP, our people, our agencies or our clients must be reported to WPP as soon as possible. Contact the Communications and/or Legal teams in the first instance. 

The WPP Communications team (working with WPP Legal and other functions as required) will be responsible for the preparation, approval and dissemination of any WPP press releases, statements, Stock Exchange announcements, posts on digital and social channels and internal messages. Agency announcements, press releases or other forms of external or internal communication involving or mentioning WPP, or that might impact on WPP in any way, must be discussed and cleared before release with the WPP Communications team. 

Only certain members of the WPP Communications team and WPP senior management team are authorised to speak to the media on behalf of WPP, or about issues that may affect WPP. Media enquiries, including requests for comment or interviews, should be referred to the WPP Communications team.

# Disclosure of confidential information

As a publicly listed company, WPP is subject to strict rules relating to the disclosure of information. It will be a disciplinary offence for any employee to disclose confidential information relating to WPP, agencies, or clients, unless required to do so in the proper execution of their duties. 

You must also follow the rules and guidelines of the internal control bulletin “Public release of financial information by Group companies” and the accompanying release “Q&As on public release of information”. These prohibit the release of financial information including revenue, financial trends and headcount to the press or similar organisations, or via social media. The principle is that this information must not enter the public domain. This policy does not, however, prohibit the filing of information required by applicable laws. 

# Approaches from investors and analysts

To ensure that WPP is correctly understood and valued in the marketplace and that publicly available information is communicated consistently and clearly to the investing public, anyone receiving direct approaches from investors in WPP, or analysts who cover WPP, must refer to the Group Investor Relations Director or WPP’s Chief Financial Officer for assistance. 

As per section 5, there are strict guidelines relating to the external disclosure of financial or business information on the Group and its agencies, and potentially severe consequences if a breach of those procedures should lead to the disclosure of inside information.
