# Introduction

(GRE) is mandated to manage and support WPP’s Global real estate processes. The GRE Global Director reports directly into the Group CFO. GRE has the following specific mandate:  

Management and delivery of the Campus Programme.

Management of WPP’s real estate policies, project initiation and approval processes.

Management of the relationship between stakeholders in business units, WPP and suppliers/vendors.

Management of capex approval process.

Management of the real estate database, reporting and audit compliance for excess space reporting.

Professional support and promotion of best practice across all areas of real estate activity within WPP, including workplace design, sustainability and colocation.

Ensure all major projects are delivered on time within budget.

Responsibility for continuous improvement in efficiency of occupation and cost across the Group’s real estate portfolio and for procuring buildings and space which provide good quality support for WPP businesses.

Providing support for Group M&A Activity.

Supporting the Group’s commitments to corporate responsibility – environmental and climate change through more efficient use of real estate.

Providing support for IFRS16 and other statutory reporting to Group companies and WPP Group Reporting.

# General policy statement

Each of the following needs approval: 

any lease, sublease, sale or purchase of real estate;

requirement for incremental space;

notification of, and disposal of excess space; 


renewal, extension and break of a lease or any substantial variation in the length of lease term or rental commitment; 

exercise of a lease break or option; 

rent review and any lease negotiation, including sharing of space with other Group companies; and 

reconfiguration or refurbishment of existing space - this may include maintenance for a building where the capex expenditure is treated as an asset rather than a P&L expense. 


When seeking approval for taking on a new lease, Group companies must be able to demonstrate that all alternatives, including the existing lease and other WPP space in the locality, have been taken into account. 


In markets where WPP has a campus or another space available, Group companies must utilise that space and should not renew existing leases or take on a new lease in the same market. Exceptions can be made only in exceptional circumstances and with the prior approval of WPP’s CFO. 


In addition to the approval from WPP, any real estate project above must also be approved by the network's CFO. It is the Group company’s responsibility to obtain such approvals.

Any Group company proposing to renew a lease or take on a new lease obligation must contact  You should contact your regional GRE team in the first instance. GRE and the WPP property advisor will negotiate or verify the terms of transactions and ensure these are in line with the local market.  


For the avoidance of any doubt no Group companies can instruct external lawyers on any real estate project. 


Upon signature of a new lease or renewal, a fully executed PDF version in the local language must be forwarded to the lease administration team within each regional GRE office. The WPP property advisor will abstract the lease’s data into the real estate data management system, CoStar, which will enable IFRS16 accounting for the lease. 


All changes during the course of a lease must also be notified to the lease administration team including the provision of rent invoices where the rent has changed; for example, where it has increased due to inflation or indexation. 


Periodically, GRE or WPP property advisor may request verification of data in accordance with an audit or SOX compliance. Group companies and their business units should supply information requested in a timely manner. 


# Timing


As part of the Project Initiation Process, a cost/benefit analysis of shortlisted buildings against the existing lease must be submitted along with the local real estate advisor’s advice on the terms and conditions and draft Heads of Terms. A recommendation must be given to WPP for approval “in principle” no later than six months prior to the date on which the lease is to be signed, extended or Notice served to break or extend. The final terms must be presented for approval at least one month before any signatories are necessary. 


# Related party transactions


Related party lease transactions are strongly discouraged as they create conflicts, which frequently obstruct reaching optimal real estate solutions. 


If such a transaction is to be undertaken, WPP will insist on due diligence being undertaken on behalf of the Group company by lawyers, GRE and the preferred real estate supplier, to ensure the terms are fully at arm’s length, and that there are no commercial conflicts of interest. The principle of signing a lease with a related party will require the express approval of either WPP’s Group CEO or CFO. 


# WPP guarantee & confidentiality


No guarantee, indemnity, letter of comfort, warranty or other representation may be given to any third party, whether a bank or otherwise, without the prior approval of the WPP Director of Treasury and WPP Legal. 


WPP’s covenant in any form should not be offered as guarantor in any capacity without the express approval of WPP. 


Any offer, Heads of Terms, lease contract or terms of engagement between parties relating to a real estate transaction, should contain a confidentiality undertaking whereby the terms of any offer made by WPP will not be made public. 


# Health & Safety


WPP Group companies are responsible for ensuring that Health & Safety and other occupational aspects comply with local legislation at a minimum, in addition to any corporate policy directives as may be issued by WPP from time to time. 


Health & Safety legislation differs from country to country. If in doubt, expert guidance for the rules that are applicable to your region should be obtained.

# Real estate capital expenditure (capex)


Capex approved for a specific project in a specific year is not transferable and can only be spent on that project in that specific year. Any amount carried over must be applied for again in the following budget year. As long as the project details remain the same, approval will be granted automatically. 


Once approved through either a or a Property Business Case approval, the capex amount must be entered in BFC. Any unapproved capex for projects over US $25,000 in BFC must be removed. GRE will review all BFC entries including those with capex less than US $25,000 to ensure this policy is adhered to. 

Note that multiple entries in BFC for the same project will be evaluated in aggregate. GRE will mandate removal of any capex from BFC not adhering to the policy. 


Real Estate capex includes the cost of: 


IT infrastructure and cabling, audio visual equipment and furniture, but other IT related items are excluded e.g. computers, computer hardware and telephone equipment. 


Project fees. Project related fees, e.g. design and project management fees can be capitalised. Brokerage and legal fees can only be capitalised if they were incurred in the acquisition of new space. Fees for lease renewals and renegotiations cannot normally be capitalised. 


All projects over US$250,000 must have a Project Manager appointed. The cost of these appointments is to be borne by the operating company and should be included in the capex application. GRE must be contacted before making any appointments in this area. 


Formal framework agreements for Project Management services are in place in most locations. These will specify the scope and cost of the services. If no such agreement is in place, GRE will advise on the basis of engagement. All agreements should be confirmed in writing. 


GRE will normally undertake a Post Project Review to ensure that the targets set in the Project Execution Plan are met and that the project has been completed within Budget. The scope and detail of the review will depend on the complexity of the project and be agreed with GRE. 


GRE and WPP Commercial & Procurement Services have, in some locations, put in place purchasing agreements with suppliers/vendors for furniture, fittings and equipment. These agreements should be utilised. Whether or not such agreements exist, competitive quotations must be obtained for construction works, furniture, fittings and equipment. GRE will advise on appropriate methods of procurement. 


All contractors and suppliers must be pre-qualified and have attained certain quality, environmental and Health & Safety requirements. They should all be required to confirm acceptance of WPP’s Ethics, Anti-Bribery, Anti-Corruption and Anti-Fraud policies contained within WPP’s Code of Conduct document. Under no circumstances should monies be paid before works are commenced and it is preferable to hold retention monies until the project is complete. GRE or your Project Manager can advise on the most appropriate course of action. 


# Campuses


The campus programme is a collaborative process involving GRE, local teams and partners. Each location is planned, agreed and approved by the transformation board before they go live. It is the responsibility of the Real Estate team to deliver in line with the approvals of the board. 


Post occupation, a governance board must be established to ensure the ongoing management of campus and should consist of business leaders, country managers and members of the Real Estate and teams. 


# Excess space: audit compliance


As part of reporting requirements, GRE is required to track excess space within the real estate portfolio. Group companies are obliged to inform their regional GRE team of excess space, and to respond in a timely manner to the annual questionnaire on headcount and other information to establish excess space to comply with WPP’s obligations to record all liabilities.
