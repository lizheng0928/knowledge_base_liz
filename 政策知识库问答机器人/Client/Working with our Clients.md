# 2. 	Client Contracts & Contract Compliance Overview

Client contracts are critical to WPP and our clients. Contracts should never be ignored or treated as a formality, even for short term or low value projects. Properly prepared client contracts ensure accurate revenue and expense recognition and reduce the potential for disputes. 

If a contract is not negotiated, business will either be conducted on implied terms, or worse, on the client’s standard purchase order terms, which will typically impose a very high level of risk on the Agency. 


# The Policy

Establishment of Contract 

Work for clients must not commence without, at a minimum: 

the Agency and the client agreeing in writing the services to be performed, an estimated project timeline, compensation and payment terms (either in the form of a signed contract, interim letter or SOW); or 

where the client has a purchase order process, a valid purchase order being issued by the client to the Agency 

A full contract (or a SOW with T&Cs attached, which have been reviewed by the Agency) must be entered into as soon as possible. Subject to approval by the Agency Regional CFO, where the client has a purchase order process, a temporary exception up to one month of work can be undertaken without a purchase order being issued. No further work should be performed without the ability to invoice, unless approved monthly by the Network Global CFO, and notified to the WPP Group Finance Director. Where work is to commence on the basis of the client’s purchase order, always agree with the client in writing that any standard terms referenced (or appended) to the purchase order will not apply and will be subject to the terms of a full form contact to be negotiated and agreed in good faith within an agreed period of time.

**3. Client Credit Policy Overview **

Agencies must ensure that prospective clients can pay for the services requested. 


# The Policy

The Agency must conduct a credit review for each prospective client prior to signing a contract or letter of intent. The client company reviewed should be the company which is signing the contract. Reviews should be conducted on all clients, including those who have a central or pre-existing relationship with the WPP Group. 

The review must include a review of balance sheets and profit and loss statements of the prospective client (where available) or a credit check with independent credit research organisations, banking institutions and other major creditors of the prospective client. 

This review must be widened to include other related or affiliated companies in the client’s group, if the Agency is providing services to those related/affiliated companies. 

If the credit review indicates a limited ability to pay, sufficient safeguards should be included in the client contract to ensure that the Agency does not undertake any risk in expenditure incurred on behalf of the client. Such safeguards might include payments in advance of the work, a parent company guarantee, payments being made directly by the client to third party suppliers, restrictive credit terms or receiving a bank letter of credit. 

If the credit review indicates a limited ability to pay and the client is not willing to provide sufficient safeguards (as described in paragraph 4), the client contract and the billing arrangements should be reviewed by the Global Network CFO. Credit terms in excess of US$50,000 to an existing client which is known to be in financial difficulty also require approval in accordance with the Agency network’s approval process. 

If the Agency is working with a Government client and a conventional credit review is not possible, work must not commence with that client without the approval of the relevant Agency/WPP Country Manager, where applicable, or Network CFO’s and WPP Legal for all other countries. The Country Manager should consider the relevant country’s sovereign credit rating as part of the approval process.  

Each Network must put in place a process to perform annual credit review or assessment of existing clients with annual spend over $1 million, to ensure the client’s ability to make payments. 

Clients in administration e.g. managed bankruptcy, chapter 11 etc. must prepay 100% of fees and third-party costs prior to work commencing.  

Agencies that are not part of the GroupM network must not buy media on behalf of clients without first obtaining WPP Group Commercial / Finance Approval and putting in place credit insurance. Agencies who are subcontracting any media buying to GroupM must also ensure that GroupM’s credit insurance covers the relevant client. 





# 4. Client Billing & Payment Terms  Overview

The management of payment terms with our clients is critical to ensure the proper cash flow for our business. Our role is to add value to our client’s business through our ideas and creativity rather than to help fund their business. When we get paid is determined by the time that we receive a client purchase order, when we are able to invoice and the date the client pays. Below are policies related to each.  


# The Policy

Purchase Orders  

A purchase order, or other such client specific approval to invoice, is required prior to work commencing. 

During the renewal of a scope of work, until fees for the renewed scope have been agreed, invoicing of a minimum of 75% of a prior year fee, or the estimated current year fee (where this is materially lower than prior year) is acceptable.  

Subject to approval by the Agency Regional CFO, a temporary exception up to 1 month of work can be undertaken without the ability to invoice. For work beyond this period, Regional CFO approval is required and can only be agreed up to a maximum of $250k. No further work should be performed without the ability to invoice, unless approved monthly by the Network Global CFO and notified to WPP Group Finance Director. 

Invoicing 

Agency fees, in the form of retainer or fixed amounts, should be billed as early in the month of service as possible, and no later than the end of the month of service. 


Project fees should be billed regularly with agreed milestones (ideally on a monthly basis) over the development and completion of the project.  

Production, media and other third-party expenses may need to be billed upfront in order to ensure alignment of payment to the third party vendor, and receipt of funds from the client. 

Payment Terms  

Any payment terms for a new client or amended payment terms for an existing client relating to **fees** must be no longer than 30 days from end of the month of service, or 60 days beginning of month of service.  On an exceptional basis, and with approval by the Network Global CFO and WPP Group Commercial CFO, payment terms of 60 days from end of the month of service can be agreed.   

Payment terms for **third party costs** and **expenses** should be made at least on a cash neutrality basis. The Agency should never be required to use its own funds for procurement or payment of client third party costs. 

Payment terms for **media costs** should be made at least on a cash neutrality basis.  

In China payment terms should run no later than from the point at which all of the services have been delivered to the client.  

The de facto method for receiving payments from clients is by bank transfer to the operating bank account of a WPP/Agency legal entity. Receiving payments in the form of cash, credit cards, factoring, shares, barter or any other method is not permitted. 

Investing or taking equity, or options to acquire equity, in clients (including in relation to services provided) would only be considered in exceptional circumstance and requires prior written approval from WPP’s CFO. No related discussion with clients is to be commenced without prior written approval from the WPP CFO. 

Accepting payment from clients in the form of future dated promissory notes, bank drafts or similar instruments, whether guaranteed by a bank or not, requires prior written approval from the network CFO and WPP Treasury.  No discussion with client is to be commenced without prior Treasury written approval. 

No discussions are to be entered into with clients for the use of any form of receivables or supply chain financing as a conduit to settle invoices without first consultation with WPP Global Client Operations, the network regional CFO and WPP Treasury. Please refer to Policy 13.9 of the   

Clients must not receive a discount for early payment of agency fees.   

Clients must receive the benefit of early payment discounts (“EPD”) actually received by the Agency on third party costs if the client pays the Agency in time and has a contractual right to receive the benefit of the Agency EPD. EPDs must only be passed to the client if there is no outstanding indebtedness with the client’s accounts. Agency must not use its own funds to secure an EPD benefit on behalf of the client.  






# 5. Client Compensation  Overview

There are many methodologies for determining compensation. All methods should ultimately lead to a profitable working relationship. Please contact the WPP Global Client Operations for further information.  


# The Policy

**Periodic review of billing rates** – Billing rates must be reviewed annually with clients to determine whether renegotiation is required to account for changes in market conditions and underlying salary and operating costs. For high inflation markets a review may be required more frequently. 

**Cost-plus arrangements** - Where fee arrangements are based on cost-plus: 

All costs, both direct and indirect, should be included when calculating fees. 

Overhead:  

The overhead rate used to calculate fees should be sufficient to recover the actual overhead of the office during the period of the engagement; and 

The Agency must include an additional allocation of WPP overhead, which is calculated as a percentage of Agency revenue, in its overhead recovery rate. This covers costs incurred directly by WPP but not recharged to the Agency and therefore not in the Agency P&L. The current year’s percentage figure is calculated by WPP and can be obtained from WPP Global Client Operations.  

**Standard time** - Standard days (and/or hours) should be no more than that specified in the employment contract, or as the standard for that Agency in the relevant market, net of holiday and other paid time off. 

**Most ****Favoured**** Nations / Most ****Favourable**** Terms** – The Agency must not give a commitment that the price offered to one client is at least as favorable as the price offered to other clients. Each client’s remuneration deal is unique and specific to that client.  While this concept is more prevalent when buying or selling commoditized goods, it is not appropriate in a services context.  No two assignments are exactly the same, as each scope of work tends to be unique and tailored to the particular client’s attributes, business challenges, ways of working, and level of engagement with the agency.   

**Documentation** - Compensation arrangements must be documented in writing prior to commencing the work. 





# 6. Audits by Clients Overview

Client audits have become more complex and extensive and we are regularly asked to provide audit rights not just to demonstrate good financial stewardship of client monies but also compliance with other contractual terms including use of personal information, confidentiality and anti-bribery & corruption. 


# The Policy

Exceptions to this policy can be made on approval by the WPP Global Client Operations and WPP Legal. 

**Audits must be according to the terms of the client** **contract** – Both the auditor and the records that are subject to audit should be clearly defined in the contract. In the absence of specific contractual audit provisions in an agreement, the client does not have an automatic right to audit. 

**Global Network CFO / Global Commercial Director approval is required for use of any auditor that is not an independent certified public accounting (CPA) firm including the “Big 4” **(Deloitte, PWC, EY and KPMG).  Client’s internal audit team may audit third-party pass-through costs. Any other non- CPA audit firm must be mutually agreed between the client and Agency. 

**Independence** - To ensure auditor independence, consultants (e.g. management or compensation consultants) are not permitted to perform financial audits or to gain access to financial records. Audit firms that we allow to perform compliance audits will not be permitted to also work as consultants. Auditors must not be remunerated on a contingency or share of findings basis. 

**Period subject to audit rights** - Audit rights should apply only to periods where contractual audit rights apply, ideally only to records from the previous 3 years and never exceeding WPP’s record retention policy of 7 years or as prescribed by local law if different. 

**Auditor must sign a non-disclosure agreement** in a form acceptable to the Agency, confirming that information obtained during the audit will be kept confidential and only used for the purpose of the audit. Data and information collected during the audit must not be used by the auditor for any other purpose including as a benchmark for its work for this or other clients, prospective clients or industry bodies. 

**The audit scope, and changes to the audit scope, must be agreed in advance between the client, the auditor and the Agency.** 

**Exclusions from right and scope of audit** – The following must always be excluded from the scope of the audit: input elements related to output-based charging, fixed pricing arrangements, individual payroll and personnel files, non-disclosed media sales, information relating to the Agency’s other clients, Agency’s overhead costs or non-billable expenses, information subject to confidentiality restrictions in the Agency’s agreements with third parties, and the Agency’s IT systems, servers and disaster recovery plans.  

Audits rights covering **confidentiality, privacy, IT, security, regulatory** **issues** and **anti-bribery & corruption **must be approved by WPP Legal. 

**Subcontractors** – Agencies should never grant audit rights over subcontractors in a client contract unless they are majority-owned or controlled by WPP. We can only agree to use reasonable efforts to attempt to provide the client audit rights over those companies or affiliates that are not majority owned or controlled by WPP. 

**Audit is a client cost** - The client is responsible for the cost of the audit, including the cost of their personnel and any third-party auditor. The exception to this is where the Agency contractually agrees to pay actual third party audit costs where a minimum amount of findings has been exceeded.  

**Expiry of audit rights** – Audit rights should ideally expire when a client contract terminates and should ideally not exceed one year post-termination of a client contract unless approved by Global Agency CFO or Global Commercial 

Director.  

**Notice period and location** – Audits should be subject to reasonable notice, ideally 30 days, although shorter notice may be granted where there are grounds to suspect fraud or breach of law or to assist the client with legal or regulatory requirements. Audits should take place during business hours and at our premises  

**Review of the draft audit report** - When the auditor is appointed by the client, the Agency should be permitted with reasonable notice e.g. 5 working days, to review and comment upon the draft findings report prior to the report being presented to the client. Where the auditor is appointed solely by WPP or the Agency, the client will receive a summary of the audit report from the Agency.  




# 7. Cross Border Billing, VAT, Sales Tax and Treasury Overview

WPP companies are often asked by clients to assist in the financial management of the client’s budget through cross-border central or hub billing. The tax and currency issues with cross border billing for any aspect of client service are complex and can create a substantial financial risk if not managed correctly.  

The related administration and Agency resource required to manage the process may also be significant and should be considered. 

For the purposes of this policy, cross-border billing refers to invoicing an entity outside of the country in which the services were performed, central billing refers to coordinated billing on behalf of the client out of one office, and hub billing applies where the client asks for all invoices to be sent to their nominated centre. ** **


# The Policy

An Agency should bill all services and reimbursable third-party costs on a local-to-local basis where possible.  

In some circumstances, a client may request central or hub billing. WPP Tax and Treasury must approve all proposals for cross-border central or hub billing. More specifically, if a US Agency is to act as a central billing location, WPP Tax (US) must be consulted in advance as there are additional tax costs to WPP (please see (6) below).  

WPP Tax must approve all cross-border billing where withholding tax is expected to apply and where annual fees (including pass through costs/vendor costs) are expected to exceed $1,000,000. Please seek guidance from WPP Tax if you are uncertain as to whether withholding tax will apply.  

Where approval is required from WPP Tax, it must be obtained before a contract is agreed with the client. Failure to notify WPP Tax prior to entering such contracts could result in significant tax charges.  

The client should be financially responsible for all indirect taxes (VAT, GST, Sales tax) and currency fluctuations. Please refer to the for further information. 

If a US Agency is acting as a central billing location on the client’s request in respect of services provided by non-US WPP companies, the client should be financially responsible for any applicable BEAT tax or should acknowledge that the US Agency is acting as agent on behalf of the non-US WPP companies. Please refer to the for further information and please consult with WPP Tax (US) in advance to ensure that any implications are fully understood. 

Where a contract will involve cross-border billing or central billing, agencies should, where possible, include the standard tax terms and conditions concerning VAT, Sales taxes and Withholding tax, that are set out within the  This is required to protect agencies from incurring unexpected tax costs on these engagements. 

Invoices should be raised in the currency in which the costs are incurred. If this is not possible the transaction should be FX neutral – i.e. the Agency should not suffer any foreign exchange gain or loss as a result of billing in a different currency.

# 8. Client Contracts and Insurance Provisions Overview

WPP’s business practice is to have appropriate levels of insurance to protect our company’s assets, our employees, and our client’s assets. Clients will often require contract terms requiring the Agency to maintain a minimum level of insurance in the areas of: 

Workers’ compensation (also called employers liability). 

General liability (also called public liability). 

Automobile insurance (not typically relevant outside the US). 

Advertiser’s liability / error & omissions liability (also called professional indemnity). 


# The Policy

1. Clients can never be added as ‘named insured’ parties on any WPP policy.  

The insured party on any insurance policy is the party entitled to make a claim. Insurance companies will not pay out claims between two named parties on the same policy. Therefore, if our client was named on our policy and a dispute arose between us, we would not be able to make a claim relating to that dispute – our cover would be void.  

What our insurance policies do contain is an ‘indemnity to principal’ clause instead. This means that if our client is sued as a result of our actions, our insurance policy can still be drawn upon to cover any losses.   

Clients can only be added as ‘additional insured’ parties on the general liability and on the automobile policies, but not on any other policy. 

Do not agree to: 

advise clients on changes or cancellation of our insurance policies; 

clients having the ability to dictate which insurers we use; 

requiring our insurer to waive their rights of subrogation (or ‘step-in rights’) against clients (our insurer will never agree this as this is the insurer’s right to sue the client directly if the client caused the loss); or   

sharing policy documents and deductible levels - this is confidential to us. We can share certificates of agreed coverage on request).  

The existing insurance policies cover all majority-owned WPP agencies that have a minimum of 50.1% direct or indirect ownership by WPP plc. WPP’s policies do not cover minority owned or affiliated companies. Minority owned or affiliated companies must obtain separate insurance coverage at their own expense. 

When negotiating global contracts with a client, specified insurance cover amounts should be noted – please contact WPP’s Group Risk Officer for guidance and approval. 


# Guidance

For all insurance provisions, the limit for insurance levels and suggested contract wording that complies with WPP’s policies is available from WPP’s Group Risk Officer. Please also refer to the Client Contract Toolkit available  WPP insurance manuals can be found  


# 9. Confidential Information Overview

WPP’s policy on confidentiality is intended to ensure agencies and their clients, suppliers and other third parties they work with, remain vigilant in the handling of confidential information and have proper protocols in place to protect confidential information. This includes ensuring that staff do not post sensitive information online or in social media or use it in their portfolios. 

Confidential information can relate to any subject matter and be stored in any form (whether hard copy, electronic or even stored in people's minds). The Agency will have its own confidential information, and will also be responsible for safeguarding client confidential information. Examples of client confidential information include a new product design, a marketing strategy and software code, business plans, media plans, management accounts, trade secrets, know-how, business and financial information and other proprietary information or data disclosed to one party by the other or incorporated in materials or products provided to one party by the other.  Examples of Agency confidential information include its remuneration models, pricing information (e.g. rate cards), pitch submissions, ways of working, employee information, information relating to other clients, IT systems and disaster recovery plans, business plans, forecasts and budgets and non-public financial accounts. 


Personal Data or PII is information that would typically fall within the categories described above and so must also be treated as confidential. 


# The Policy General

Confidentiality agreements (also called non-disclosure agreements or NDAs) must be used wherever confidential information is being disclosed or received. If an appropriate NDA is not available, WPP Legal can provide one.  

Unless approved by WPP Legal in advance, NDAs must:  

be mutually binding on both parties where information is flowing both ways.   

not contain: 

Any transfer of Agency copyright or any other intellectual property (IP) rights to the other party;   

Any restriction on the Agency’s ability to work for competitors of the other party (or anyone else); 

Any restriction on the Agency’s ability to engage staff; or 

Financial penalties or liquidated damages which kick in automatically if there is a breach. 

Confidential information must not be disclosed to parties not permitted by the NDA, directly or indirectly, without obtaining the prior written consent of all parties having a potential interest. 

Access to and use of confidential information must be secure (e.g. physical and/or logical separation of client confidential information, separation of client teams, confidentiality training).  

Employees must never sign NDAs directly with clients. NDAs must always be signed by the Agency who is responsible to the client for compliance by its employees.  

If there has been a breach of confidentiality, notify your line manager and WPP Legal immediately.  Any delay in notification can have implications under WPP’s insurance policies.  

New Business 

Mutual NDAs must always be put in place with prospective clients, and any third-party procurement and pitch consultants.  

The Agency’s pricing information and creative ideas are confidential and must be protected. All materials presented should be clearly marked with a copyright and confidentiality notice (see example below).  If you are not successful in the pitch, the prospective client should not be entitled to use any work presented without paying a fee.

No part of this information may be disclosed to others, nor may it be used, reproduced, copied or transmitted in any form or by any means, electronic, mechanical, or otherwise without prior written permission of the Agency, and this document/presentation must be returned immediately upon the Agency’s request. *

Agencies must not disclose any of the following information to clients or prospective clients (even if there is an NDA in place): 

Non-public financial information  

Information on other clients  

The fact the Agency is working with any other client (unless this is publicly available information or the other client has consented in writing).  

Agency revenue and headcount (this should only be disclosed in broad bands – e.g less than 10m, 10-25m, greater than 200 etc.)  




# 10. Client Conflicts and Competitor Restrictions  Overview

It is common for clients to seek to limit the Agency’s ability to work for competitors of the client. This is a restrictive practice which could severely limit the Agency’s ability to generate revenue and drive growth. The client’s perspective is that they want to avoid any possible disclosure of their confidential information in order to maintain and / or enhance their position in the market.  

WPP’s perspective is that there are several possible ways of ensuring that the client’s interests are appropriately protected which would not restrict an Agency’s ability to provide services to competitors.   

It is important to recognise that avoiding overly restrictive non-compete terms allows Agency employees to develop industry, brand or therapeutic category expertise which is highly valued by our clients. 


# The Policy

Agencies should avoid severe restrictions on their ability to work for competitors of the client. Confidentiality provisions in client contract and physical separation of teams working on competing products will typically provide the appropriate safeguards to maintain a high level of trust with our clients and minimize the chance for unintentional disclosures of confidential information. 

If restrictions cannot be avoided, Agencies should only agree to restricting key individuals who are working on the client’s account from working with any client competitor. The restrictions should not apply to or any support staff (e.g. Finance, IT and HR) or client employees who are non-dedicated (e.g. senior management). 

Global and/or regional restrictions must not be accepted without the approval of the Agency network’s respective global and/or regional CFO or CEO.  

Restrictions must not apply to production services provided by third parties or Hogarth, and syndicated market research services.   

Restrictions must not bind employees from a different network without approval by that Agency network’s senior management and the WPP Global Client Team.  

Where an Agency agrees to conflict management principles (such as physical/logical separation of client information and ‘cooling off’ periods for key employees), it must implement and adhere to them. 

Restrictive employment obligations on Agency employees are prohibited.** **
