# Introduction


It is WPP’s policy to comply with the antitrust laws of all jurisdictions where WPP carries on business. These are laws and regulations that are designed to promote competition by ensuring that market power is not improperly concentrated and prevent firms from colluding to limit competition.    


Antitrust laws affect almost every area of WPP’s business and severe consequences, including significant civil and criminal penalties (up to 10% of global turnover per breach) may result from antitrust violations, both for WPP and any individuals involved.  


# Conduct


Examples of antitrust breaches would include: 

Price Fixing: Agreements with competitors that establish prices or other terms upon which services will be provided. 


Bid Rigging: Agreements between competing companies to co-ordinate their response to invitations to pitch for a particular contract, for example from a government agency or a large company asking competing agencies to pitch. Bid Rigging would include a scenario where competing agencies may seek to agree which agency will win a given tender, and agree that the other will not bid, or will submit a higher bid. 


Market Sharing: Agreements with competitors that determine which of the parties will serve particular geographic areas, product markets or clients. 


Exclusionary Conduct: Attempts, undertaken alone or with others, to drive a competitor out of business, to exclude a competitor from a geographic area or product market, or to prevent new competition.   


Other Conduct: Agreements between competitors not to hire each other’s employees or approach each other’s clients. 



Information Sharing: Sharing of confidential data (either directly or through trade associations) runs a risk of breaching antitrust provisions. This could include discussions of pricing, margin, terms and conditions, rebates, discounts, strategy, research, or client lists and could apply to information about clients or suppliers.   

Any requests from competitors or others to discuss these practices or any other information which could be anti-competitive must be declined and referred to WPP Legal immediately.   



# Agreements within WPP Group


Although antitrust provisions do not generally apply to agreements between different subsidiary agencies within the same group, such as WPP – even if they compete against each other on a day-to-day basis – this does not mean that group companies can necessarily exchange client information of any sort (including but not limited to information regarding prices, for example) or co-ordinate bids. Such exchanges may violate existing confidentiality obligations or a client’s pitch procedures. Client confidentiality is of paramount importance. To the extent you have any questions about what type of coordination within WPP is permissible with respect to client pitches you should contact WPP Legal.
