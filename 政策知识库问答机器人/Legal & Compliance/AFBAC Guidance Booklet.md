# 1. 	INTRODUCTION


**WPP DOES NOT TOLERATE FRAUD, BRIBERY OR CORRUPTION. FRAUD AND BRIBERY ARE ILLEGAL. **


**What is fraud? **

Fraud involves the use of deceit or trickery to gain an inappropriate advantage, often financial, over another person or business. Fraud can exist even if there is no dishonesty – a lower level of knowledge, for example, suspicion or recklessness of any person (a business partner or a supplier, not necessarily only another employee), can be enough to trigger criminal liability. So it is important to speak up about behaviour at odds with WPP’s Code of Business Conduct or AFBAC policy. 

Fraud itself is a criminal offence under the UK Economic Crime and Transparency Act which has global reach.  


**What is a bribe? **

A bribe is the giving or promising, or requesting or accepting, or agreeing to accept, a financial or other advantage (i.e. ‘something of value’) to induce a person to improperly perform a function or activity. That function or activity can be of a public nature or connected with business or any third party, – it could be performed during employment or on behalf of a business or a third party. 

Bribes are a criminal offence under both the UK Bribery Act and the US Foreign Corrupt Practices 

Act and under the laws of many of the jurisdictions where WPP operates – and these laws apply to our businesses and people globally. UK and US regulators can and do prosecute acts of bribery and corruption that happen outside of the UK and the US. 

Individuals engaging in fraud or giving, authorizing, or taking bribes (or promising to do so) may face fines and prison and WPP may be investigated, prosecuted, fined and/or barred from government work for such misconduct. 

WPP must have procedures in place that are reasonably designed to prevent fraud and bribery, whether in our subsidiaries, associates, affiliates or by any other party working on our behalf including freelancers, vendors and suppliers. This document sets out these procedures. 

This guidance is relevant to every single person working within WPP. It is not aimed at our directors and senior management only, nor it is solely for the attention of the finance teams. Failure to comply with anti-fraud, bribery and corruption laws or WPP policies may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of one’s employment. 


# 2. THE LAW


## 2.1 	UK BRIBERY ACT

The UK Bribery Act is the most comprehensive statement of anti-bribery legislation and it has an extensive jurisdictional reach. 

It prohibits ALL bribes, not just bribes to government officials, and makes them a criminal offence (whether the bribe involves giving, offering, promising, or receiving something of value), regardless of whether the bribe is carried out in the UK or overseas. 

Facilitation payments are also prohibited. 

It contains not only offences relating to those involved in the bribe, but also includes an offence applicable to all corporates (including WPP) which may make a corporate liable for failing to prevent a bribe. 

The scope of the legislation is wide enough to capture a bribe carried out by someone whilst performing a service on behalf of WPP or one of its operating companies. As such, liability could extend beyond subsidiaries of WPP to its JVs, associates, affiliates, brokers and agents (i.e. to people and entities we have no control over). 


## 2.2 	US FOREIGN CORRUPT PRACTICES ACT (FCPA)

The FCPA states that no money or thing of value be offered, paid, authorized, given or promised, directly or indirectly, to a Government Official in any country outside the US to corruptly influence his or her actions in order to obtain or retain business or to secure improper advantage. Note that a bribe does not need to be in the form of cash – it could be a gift, entertaining, a car, a holiday or any benefit, such as providing an internship (even an unpaid one) or job to a family member, etc. It is a criminal offence just to promise a bribe – even if it is never paid. A payment or transfer of value to a third party (such as a supplier) who is not a foreign government official on the understanding that the value will be transferred to a government official is also a criminal offence.  

The term “Government Official” is broadly defined and applies to all employees, officers, officeholders, and agents, of any level of seniority, of a state agency, department or company owned or controlled in whole or in part by a government entity, as well as political party officials, candidates for office, and employees of public international organizations. This includes all levels of government, including federal, national, local, state, province, city or county government.   

This includes members of nationalized or state-owned institutions (which may include banks, utilities, telephone companies, railways, tourist authorities, public universities, and public hospitals etc.). 


## 2.3 	UK ECONOMIC CRIME AND CORPORATE TRANSPARENCY ACT (ECCTA)

Fraud: 

The UK ECCTA holds WPP and subsidiaries liable where a fraud offence is committed by an associated person with intent to benefit the Group of our clients. Fraud is a criminal offence with global reach. 

An associated person can include an employee, agent, freelancer, contractor, vendor, supplier or subsidiary.  

The only defence is to prove that WPP had reasonable prevention procedures in place. Fines are unlimited if convicted. 

If committed by an employee, most economic crimes – including fraud, bribery, money laundering, sanctions breaches, fraud and false accounting – only require the involvement of a “senior manager“ under the UK ECCTA. Knowledge of the board of directors or of individual directors is not required.  

“Senior Manager” definition (section 196(4), ECCTA): an individual who “plays a significant role” in the making of decisions about how the whole or a substantial part of a company is managed or organised, or the actual managing or organising of a business. 


## 2.4 	OTHER LEGISLATION

Most countries also have their own anti-fraud, bribery and corruption laws. We all need to be aware of and comply with them. 


**3.**** ****FRAUD, BRIBERY AND CORRUPTION RISKS - WHERE DO THEY ARISE? **


## 3.1 	SEEKING CLIENT WORK, INCLUDING FROM GOVERNMENT CLIENTS

WPP companies routinely compete for new contracts via tender processes. Some of these new contracts involve significant potential billings and revenues. Some involve government organizations where there is a heightened risk if a Government Official solicits a bribe in connection with the tender process. 

Providing or offering any benefit or anything of value with the intention of improperly influencing the procurement process may constitute commercial or government bribery. Gaining an improper advantage through such behaviour, including any work won as a result, would likely also amount to a fraud. Examples of prohibited conduct include: bribes made directly, in cash, benefits such as job opportunities for an individual’s friends or family members, providing excessive gifts and entertainment, or paying for travel expenses.  

WPP’s Procurement Policy, AFBAC Policy, Business Policy, and WPP Policy Book requirements on profit-sharing arrangements should all be followed.**  ** 


## 3.2 	MAKING PAYMENTS OR HOLDING BUDGETS ON BEHALF OF CLIENTS

It is possible that a client may request an agency to deliver gifts or make payments on their behalf outside the normal assignment for the client. A client could also request an agency to retain a fund from which those gifts would be paid for or payments would be made based on their instructions. This type of transaction is prohibited and is outside the provision of your usual services to the client. You do not know what the payments are for, who the recipient is or what they will do with the gifts or payments. You may be complicit in corruption, or, at a minimum, assisting the client to breach their internal policies. 

To prevent this, the WPP Procurement Policy and WPP Gifts, Entertainment & Hospitality Policy should be followed as well as the requirements regarding authorisation of payments. 

It is also possible that a client may request an agency to hold unused budget at the completion of a project or deliverable, in the expectation that it will be used in the future for another piece of work. Holding this budget without a clear idea of what it will be used for and when could be seen as fraud. Any such request should be agreed with the agency’s regional CFO and monitored and reviewed on a quarterly basis. 


## 3.3 	GOVERNMENT TOUCHPOINTS

Wherever WPP agencies or their agents are interacting with Government Officials, such as a tax authority official, customs or immigration official, building inspectors, local police or fire authorities, or obtaining any license or permit for a WPP agency or a client of a WPP agency, there is a risk, particularly in high-risk jurisdictions for the potential of corruption.   

When engaging a third-party to interact with a government official on behalf of a WPP agency, the compensation arrangement should be reviewed to ensure that the payment structure and amount is commensurate with the services to be performed. You cannot engage any third party to win or retain government work – see WPP’s Business Adviser Policy for more details. 


## 3.4 	FACILITATION PAYMENTS

WPP, and its agencies and employees are prohibited from making facilitation payments.  Facilitation payments are payments typically made to low-level Government Officials for routine services to which the payor is normally entitled. Any benefit or payment paid, directly or indirectly, to a Government Official may be a facilitation payment. Such payments may be requested in connection with obtaining permits, licenses, permissions (such as in connection with events), clearing goods through customs, immigration and visa processing.  Even if a facilitation payment is made by a third-party on behalf of an agency, it is prohibited. 


## 3.5 	CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

If an employee allows their own interests to affect their objectivity when working for WPP, this can create a risk of fraud or corruption. For example, having an investment in a competitor and using confidential information about WPP to benefit that interest or investment; or having a personal interest in a business that could be a vendor or supplier of a WPP agency (such as a relative in a position where they would benefit from WPP giving them work) and encouraging their agency to choose the vendor or supplier without disclosing their interest – please note that the existence of a conflict of interest is not a problem per se and almost always can be managed, but it can only be managed if it is disclosed and approved in accordance with WPP’s Conflicts of Interest Policy – and not disclosing a conflict or obtaining the right approvals to proceed with a business decision regardless of a conflict is a policy breach which can carry significant consequences both for the business and the individuals involved. 




## 3.6	GIFTS, ENTERTAINMENT & HOSPITALITY

There is the potential for misuse of gifts and entertainment to improperly influence another party, particularly when pitching for new work. The WPP Gifts, Entertainment & Hospitality Policy should be followed and tailored to the local market. 



## 3.7 	LOBBYING

Certain of WPP’s businesses in the public relations sector involve lobbying. Inherently there is a risk that cash or other things of value could pass in order to influence third parties. 

US federal, state, and local laws may prohibit or limit payments, gifts or hospitality or campaign contributions to Government Officials, even where there is no intent to improperly influence the official. Theshould be followed. 


## 3.8 	ABUSE OR MISUSE OF REBATES

There is a risk of abuse or misuse of rebates. Rebates should either be transferred to the client if the contract or local law requires it or retained by the agency or a media broker. If rebates are not accounted for properly, they could be used to fund bribery or fraud or pay third party consultancy or research fees that in turn are used to pay bribes. 


## 3.9 	ASSOCIATES, AFFILIATES, AND JOINT VENTURES

The WPP Group includes a large number of JV and associate companies – generally those in which WPP owns a stake of between 20% and 50%. There are also affiliate relationships where WPP has no ownership in the affiliate, but which may allow the affiliate to trade under a WPP or agency name. 

By definition, WPP does not control these entities. However, under the UK Bribery Act and the UK ECCTA, WPP may be responsible for ensuring that these entities trade properly and are not involved in bribery. The US FCPA requires that WPP uses good faith efforts to ensure that these organisations have a system of controls consistent with WPP’s own policies to prevent bribery, and WPP may be liable under the FCPA if it has knowledge of corrupt activity by affiliates acting on behalf of WPP entities.   


## 3.10 ACQUISITIONS

Post-acquisition of an entity by WPP, WPP will be responsible for any breach of anti-bribery laws and may be financially responsible for any bribery undertaken by such entity that occurred prior to its acquisition. 

Agencies need to be aware that an acquisition structured as an earnout may create an incentive for the earnout company to win business “at any cost” during the earnout period. Similar incentives may exist for minority shareholders in WPP companies who are entitled to dividend distributions and are involved in winning new business. Agencies must ensure that newly acquired companies have the resourcing and guidance needed to adopt WPP controls and policies from the date of acquisition. 




## 3.11	GEOGRAPHICAL VARIANCE

Transparency International publishes a well-respected annual list of approximately 180 countries rating their perceived propensity to corruption (“Corruption Perception Index”). 

WPP companies operate in over 110 countries – many of these are perceived to have a far higher risk of bribery and corruption than others. You should be aware of where your country ranks as well as the ranking of those countries where you do business. WPP agencies implement policies and training designed to prevent bribery or corruption. It is mandatory for these policies to be complied with and for the training to be completed in every jurisdiction in which WPP operates. 


# 4. 	THE RAMIFICATIONS OF FRAUD, BRIBERY & CORRUPTION

There are serious implications both for WPP and individuals if they engage in bribery and/or corruption: 

Huge fines can be levied (10s or 100s of millions of dollars and potentially unlimited for the UK criminal offence of fraud) on corporations, and any profits obtained as a result of bribery have to be disgorged. 

Our reputation for sound ethics and corporate governance would be severely tarnished. That would likely impact our share (stock) price and our ability to pitch for, win new clients or retain existing clients. 

The actual process of an investigation could seriously impede the ability of an agency to continue its day-to-day operations properly. 

WPP agencies could be banned from all work for the relevant authority – for example, the EU or the UK government could ban us from all work for them for a period of time. The UK government is the UK’s largest advertiser. The impact on our P&L and on jobs and our share price could be very significant. 

There are personal ramifications too which can range from company action, such as impact on bonus or other compensation elements or disciplinary action including formal warnings or termination of employment, to external regulator and police action such as fines or imprisonment.  


# 5. 	MEASURES TO PREVENT FRAUD, BRIBERY & CORRUPTION


WPP’s only defence to the criminal offence of failing to prevent bribery under the UK Bribery Act or fraud under the UK ECCTA is to demonstrate that WPP has adequate and reasonable procedures in place to prevent bribery and fraud.  

These include: 

Top level commitment 

Risk assessment 

Due diligence 

Communication and Training 

Monitoring and Review 




## 5.1	TOP LEVEL COMMITMENT

Top level commitment is set by the Board, with this tone communicated throughout our agency networks, by all Network Risk Committees and leadership in general including CEOs, COOs, CFOs, CPOs, and Media Trading, Media Investment, Creative and Production leads.  

All business leaders must set the tone by their own conduct, by the behaviour they demand of their teams and by not turning a blind eye to issues in whatever country they are operating. They must encourage transparency and openness and make it easy for staff to raise matters of concern in confidence.  

Business leaders are also responsible for escalating any areas of concern to network management, Network Risk Committees, and/or WPP Business Integrity, as needed. 

In addition, WPP’s Code of Business Conduct specifically states that “…we respect national laws and any other laws with an international reach, such as the UK Bribery Act, UK Economic Crime & Corporate Transparency Act and the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, and the UK Modern Slavery Act, where relevant, and industry codes of conduct…We will not tolerate fraud, nor will we give, offer or accept bribes, whether in cash or otherwise, to or from any third party, including but not restricted to government officials, clients and brokers or their representatives. We will collectively ensure that all staff understand this policy through training, communication and by example”. 

You must ensure adherence to the Code by your staff – and collate and report any concerns or exceptions promptly to WPP Business Integrity. 


## 5.2 	RISK ASSESSMENT

Both WPP’s annual Business Integrity Risk Assessment and Business Risk Maps assess the likelihood and impact of a range of risks including AFBAC risk. The results of the Business Integrity Risk Assessment are used to flag risk concerns and provide recommendations for the Network Risk Committees to implement partnered with WPP Business Integrity. 

Everyone is accountable for identifying and managing risks. You, your networks and agencies must recognise AFBAC risks in day-to-day management and the examples described in this booklet provide non-exhaustive guidance as to where risks might exist. As a minimum:  

Be aware of the principal risks and uncertainties published in WPP’s Annual Report & Accounts  and how to monitor and mitigate them 

Be aware of local risks and understand what is recognised in your local Business Risk Map and what might be new, threatened or emerging risks and how all should be monitored and mitigated

## 5.3 	DUE DILIGENCE

Conducting risk-based and proportionate due diligence on acquisition targets, clients, suppliers, vendors, advisers, and joint venture partners is required and should be conducted in a manner consistent with WPP Policies, including the WPP Procurement Policy, AFBAC Policy, Business Adviser Policy and Legal Policy. 



## 5.4	COMMUNICATION AND TRAINING

Everyone is responsible for championing and implementing policy. The repercussions of not doing so can affect us all – see paragraph 4 above on ramifications. 

Network Risk Committees are responsible for identifying, monitoring and managing all risks and uncertainties for their agencies and supporting risk management at regional and local level. 

Networks must appoint named senior individuals as principal contacts and leaders in their regions and Network Risk Committees should ensure that such leaders communicate the policies and continue the tone from the top. Global, regional and local leaders should also help to ensure that all staff know how to and are comfortable to speak up, whether to line managers, People/HR or through the Right to Speak line which is available to allow reporting, including anonymously if that is preferred, of any matters of concern. 

Regular communications from management demonstrating the top level WPP commitment and the network management commitment to compliance with AFBAC laws and policies are a core component of WPP’s Business Integrity programme and increasingly a question asked by clients and a standard to which we must also hold our supply chain. 

Training is a vital part of implementation. WPP’s How We Behave online training must be completed by all staff upon joining and thereafter on an annual basis. This includesall employees and any others working for WPP for four or more weeks in aggregate. Additional trainings are conducted regularly by WPP and its networks on AFBAC risks and related topics that target specific regions, businesses or functions.
