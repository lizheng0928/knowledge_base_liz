Introduction
Under the WPP Business Adviser Policy an advis er is defined as: ‘‘…any proposed intermediary or third party who
introduces new clients, provides assistance with the retention of existing clients, makes introductions to third parties who
could assist with ongoing business or new business wins, or provides advice on particular sectors or business types, or
offers to do any of the foregoing .’’ The policy goes on to make it clear that WPP agencies cannot work with advisers in
connection with any government business .
Under the Business Adviser Policy ‘government business ’ refers to any work, project, or contract where the client would
be considered a Government Official as defined in WPP’s AFBAC Policy: all bodies, employees, officers, officeholders, and
agents, of any level of seniority, of a state agency, department or c ompany owned, controlled or funded in whole or in
part by a government entity, as well as political party officials, candidates for office, and employees of public internation al
organisations. This includes all levels of government, including federal, nati onal, local, state, province, city or county
government.
There are a number of different ways in which we may have the opportunity to work with partners to make an offer to
provide services to a government business. Given the breadth of the definition of advisers under WPP Business Advis er
Policy it is important to put guardrails around how we work with other businesses in connection with winning and servicing
such clients.
NOTE: see the Compliance Briefing entitled ‘ Paying Non -WPP Agencies (including commission & rebate share
arrangements) ’ for guidance on servicing non -government clients with non -WPP creative agencies or acting as a paying
agent for them (e.g. via a share of our commission).
Note that this briefing does NOT apply to the following circumstances:
• Direct approaches from a government department or state -owned enterprise representative ;
• Direct Invitations from government departments or state -owned enterprises to tender as part of public pitch .
In both these circumstances, it will be necessary to seek pre -approval for the opportunity by means of the Client
Acceptance Request Portal available HERE. Where seeking approval as a result of a direct approach without a formal pitch
process, please include in your Request for Approval full details of how the request was made to you and disclose any
related parties or prior relationships (commercial and pe rsonal) with the representative and relevant organisation.
Scenarios and process / guardrails
There are 3 different ways in which we might work with an other business to pitch for government clients. Please review
the guardrails for the specific relationship you are considering :
A. Joint submission in an integrated creative and media pitch with a WPP business
B. Joint submission in an integrated creative and media pitch with a NON -WPP business
C. Appointment under a contract with a NON-WPP business as a subcontractor , partner or vendor for services ultimately
provided to the government client.
A. Joint submission in an integrated creative and media pitch with a WPP business
When working with an entity that is ultimately owned (minority or majority) by WPP Plc. to pitch for a government client
it is important that the entities define the responsibilities between them . Specifically, they must address who will be
responsible as the lead or contracting entity, who will hold the financial responsibilities toward the client and who will
ensure compliance with any necessary WPP and WPP Media policy requirements including submission for approval
pursuant to the Client Acceptance Polic y and conducting client due diligence .
If the WPP agencies are pitching as part of a consortium , all NON-WPP consortium members must have been pre -approved
following the process set out below (Approved Partners ).
B. Joint submissions in an integrated creative and media pitch with a NON-WPP business
These requirements apply when a WPP company proposes to jointly submit a proposal to provide services to a government
client with a non-WPP business. A non-WPP business may be any corporate entity that is not ultimately owned (minority
or majority) by WPP Plc.

WPP companies may only agree to submit joint proposals with an Approved Partner . Prior to making a joint submission ,
the WPP company and the Approved P artner must enter into a partner agreement setting out their respective
responsibilities in relation to pitch submission and key aspects of their future partnership if awarded the work.
In the joint submission , the WPP company must fulfil the following 3 mandatory conditions :
1. be clearly and unambiguously named in the tender / pitch materials which are submitted as a participating company ;
2. have a clearly defined contribution to the proposed scope of services within the submission ;
3. have had final approval over the proposal in respect of services that it will deliver .
Where the joint submission is successful, and the partners are awarded the work t he preferred arrangement with
government clients is that each of the WPP business and the Approved Partner is a contracting partner with the
government entity , either:
a) under one multi -party contract with the government (potentially as a consortium) e.g. a tri -partite agreement ; or
b) under direct individual contracts between each service provider and the government client, e.g. one contract
between Approved Partner and Government and second contract between WPP Business and Government .
In circumstances where the government will only contract with one named party and the proposed contacting party is the
Approved Partner , please follow the subcontracting requirements in section C below .
C. Appointment as a subcontractor to a NON -WPP business
A WPP company may not be appointed as a subcontractor for the provision of services for the benefit of a government
client unless the main contractor is an Approved Partner .
Any subcontract with an Approved Partner MUST contain the following provisions:
1. Assurances from the main contractor that it will fully comply with:
a. the obligations of the main contract with the government
b. all relevant anti -bribery and corruption legislation
c. all other applicable laws
2. An obligation on the Approved Partner to declare any related parties with the government.
3. A warranty that the contract between Approved Partner and WPP Business reflects the terms of the main contract in
respect of media costs and fee structure .
4. Clear and unambiguous obligations relating to the management of rebates, discounts and other financial matters.
5. An obligation on the Approved Partner to cooperate with any investigation in relation to allegations of financial
mismanagement, fraud, ABC issues in connection with the provision of services to the government client.
Any subcontract with an Approved Partner must NOT contain any provisions with the following effects:
1. Any fee, commission or similar payable , for any purposes , by the WPP company to the Approved Partner ( for the
avoidance of doubt , the WPP Company may invoice the Approved Partner for any costs and fees which are to be
charged on a pass-through basis to the government client ).
2. A prohibition on providing information to the government , including any confidentiality provisions which may prevent
sharing information with the ultimate client (unless approved by WPP legal) .
3. Any rights of audit which go beyond the audit rights in the main contract with the government .
Approved Partner Process
Markets who want to have the opportunity to pitch for government clients should seek pre -approval of the NON -WPP
companies with whom they may wish to partner. Once a partner is approved the market may work on multiple
opportunities with that Approved Partner without seeking specific further approval each time. However, if there is a
material change of circumstances for the Approved P artner, e.g. new management or ownership or a reputational issue,
the Approved P artner should be re -submitted through the approval process.
Requests for approval or re-approval must be submitted attaching the following information as defined in the Appendix :
• Due diligence as per WPP Business Adviser Policy (see Appendix 1 for summary) ;
• Partner Information Form -- -completed by the NON-WPP proposed Partner;
• Partner Approval Form -- -completed by WPP Media OpCo locally.

Where due diligence identifies areas of concern, local management must confirm that the associated risks can be
appropriately mitigated before the request is submitted for further approval. The Partner Approval Form must summarise
any adverse findings and the mitigating actions proposed or implemented, such as further market intelligence checks
through an independent external provider , references , etc. with Legal and Risk & Controls consulted as needed. The due
diligence documentation and completed Partner Information Form must be reviewed by the market WPP Media CFO and
Legal.
The approval process is bottom -up and sequential, i.e. all Partners must be approved by (1) WPP Media Market CFO and
CEO; (2) WPP Media Regional CFO; and (3) WPP Media Global CFO (or Delegate) . Legal and Risk & Controls teams must be
consulted as necessary.
Please note that the submission of pitch proposals and signing of subcontracts with a NON -WPP company is not approved
until you receive a written confirmation from WPP Media Global CFO .
