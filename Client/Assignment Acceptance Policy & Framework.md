POLICY
Each agency must implement a client assignment acceptance review and referral process for potentially sensitive work.
Agency leadership must ensure that proposed work does not conflict with the WPP Code of Conduct or WPP policies and has been
considered against the WPP Assignment Acceptance Framework (see following pages).
Guided by the Framework, agency leadership must determine whether proposed new assignments should be referred to their
agency’s risk committee or equivalent body for review and, where necessary, escalated to WPP.
The risk committees should also review existing client lists on a regular basis and at least once a year, to assess client risk profiles
and whether those profiles have changed in such a way that would impact the ability of the company to continue working with
them.

KNOW YOUR CLIENT
Before participating in a pitch for a new client and/or undertaking any work for or on behalf of a new client, each agency must know
the identity of the person or organisation ultimately commissioning the work and have conducted the required due diligence to be
able to work for or on behalf of that client. WPP companies must:
• Use Nexis Diligence+ to conduct due diligence of the prospect and/or client. The app is available in My Apps Dashboard
• Additionally, conduct a desk-top review for red flag issues (e.g. reputational risks, controversies or corruption issues) and include
any issues arising from this search in the referral process to the risk committees.
• Request full company details (full company name and ultimate parent company) and conduct a credit review and determine
whether the client is State-Owned or a Government body and if a specific RFP process must be followed. See Policy 5.3 in the
WPP Policy Book for more information.
• Via Nexis Diligence+ conduct a search of the OFAC/UK/EU sanctions lists to confirm that the proposed client is not subject to any
sanctions.
• Ensure no business is undertaken, whether directly or indirectly, in or for any sanctioned countries including Russia, Cuba, Iran,
Yemen, North Korea and Syria, and as sanctions are introduced from time to time. A new client cannot be accepted if any of the
services are being provided in any of these markets.
• Ensure the Advisor Payment Policy is fully complied with – see link here – no work can be undertaken or contracts agreed where
an advisor or third party is the contracting party or sub-contracting any work in relation to a State-Owned or Government Client.
• Check if Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA -US) or Foreign Influence Registration Scheme (FIRS – UK), usually triggered when
a foreign power tries to influence another foreign power, is required.
• If the new client is the local office of a global client, the relevant Global Client Lead must be notified (a list of Global Client Leads
can be found on InsideWPP).

FRAMEWORK: PRINCIPLES
• The guidelines are designed for agency leaders and their risk committees
• Most decisions should be made at an agency level
• The escalation process is designed to keep WPP informed and/or consulted when necessary
• The guidelines apply to internal and other non-client-facing work as well as client work
• They should also be followed during new company acquisitions; framework to be applied during due diligence and picked up as
an action point during integration

OUR STARTING POINT
We believe that clients have the right to communicate their views and market their products and services, provided doing so
is lawful and complies with all applicable regulations and standards, and our own Code of Business Conduct. We are
committed to acting ethically in all aspects of our business and to maintaining the highest standards of honesty and integrity.
All the advice we provide to our clients and the content we produce for them must meet rigorous standards and we will not
undertake work which is intended to mislead or deceive.
This framework is designed to assist decision-making when taking on new clients, assignments and work, and assessing the
levels of sensitivity and risk associated with them. Examples mentioned throughout these pages are illustrative and non-
exhaustive.

WORKING WITH ENERGY CLIENTS
TO BE CONSIDERED ALONGSIDE OTHER CRITERIA WITHIN THIS FRAMEWORK
At WPP we support the aims of the Paris Agreement and believe in the urgent need to transition to net zero. Energy
companies1 must meet the energy needs of the world today while playing a central role in an orderly transition, if a
transformation of this scale, complexity and impact is to be successful. Our agencies work with a number of clients in
the energy sector. We believe it is essential that the communications of energy companies are truthful, fair and
accurate. We require rigorous standards to be applied to the content we produce for our energy clients, as we do for
all clients, and seek to fairly represent their actions and commitments at all times. Our policy is not to take on any client
work, including lobbying, designed to frustrate the objectives of the Paris Agreement.
When considering assignments for energy clients, we give particular attention to whether:
• The proposed work is truthful, fair and accurate
• The proposed assignment is designed to influence public policy decision-makers such as government officials
• The client has made a public commitment to the transition, and is seen to be developing transition plans
• The work relates to the approval or development of new greenfield oil and gas fields
• The work relates to the extension or development of particularly high-emission forms of energy, such as coal, or
forms of extraction that have a higher environmental impact
This is an area that we keep under ongoing review, as we continuously evaluate the nature of the work we perform for
clients and the context in which it occurs.
¹ ‘Energy companies’ in this context refers to those involved in the extraction, production, sales or distribution of oil, natural gas, coal or other fossil fuels, utility companies making use of such fuels and any relevant trade associations or organisations
