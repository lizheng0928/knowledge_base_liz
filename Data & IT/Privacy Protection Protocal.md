THE DEFAULT POSITION, IN LINE WITH WPP POLICY
IS THAT WE DO NOT TAKE IN PERSONAL DATA
WHERE THERE IS ANY OTHER OPTION.
FOR EXAMPLE, IF YOUR CLIENT CAN PROCESS THE
DATA THEMSELVES, OR TRANSFER DIRECTLY TO A
THIRD PARTY, WE SHOULD INFORM THEM HOW TO
DO THIS, WITHOUT TAKING IN THE DATA
OURSELVES.
ONLY IF THIS IS NOT POSSIBLE, SHOULD WE TAKE
RECEIPT OF THE DATA AND COMPLETE THE PRIVACY
PROTECTION PROTOCOL.

IF YOUR CLIENT ASKS YOU TO TAKE RECIEPT OF DATA THEN ASK
YOURSELVES THE FOLLOWING:
• Is the data personal data under relevant data protection laws? If you are unsure of this, please ask your local Legal teamor email
Privacy@GroupM.com
• Do you need to process the data to be able to perform the service offered?
• Who will you be releasing the data to?
• What security measures e.g. user access restrictions are you planning to implement to protect the data whilst you have access toit?
• What paperwork e.g. Contract, SOW, MS etc are you working under, please ensure that sufficient data protection clauses exit.

PRIVACY PROTECTION PROTOCOL
DSP SEATS
Clients are sometimes unable to upload Personal Data directly into a DSP seat and the Agency does it for them.
In this situation the Agency will be processing the Personal Data on behalf of the client by transferring the Personal Data to
the DSP. Once the Personal Data has been uploaded into the DSP seat we are no longer the Processor and we become a third
party.
The DSP picks up the responsibility of being the Processor once they receive the Personal Data from the Agency.
Please Note: Agency should only be doing this for the client if the client is actually unable to transfer the Personal Data
directly into the DSP, and not because they simply don’t want to.
In this instance we should complete the PPP the cover the intake and transfer of data.

PRIVACY PROTECTION PROTOCOL
COMPETITIONS
Agencies sometimes help clients run and organize a competition/prize give away. Depending on the form in which this
competition takes, clients are not always able to choose the winner themselves, and so the Agency does it for them.
In this situation the Agency will choose the winner and either (a) pass the winner’s details over to the client or (b) send the
winner the prize which has been given to the Agency by the client to issue. The Agency will be the processor of the
competition winners contact details in both these situations.
In this instance we should complete the PPP to cover passing the data to the client.

PRIVACY PROTECTION PROTOCOL
DATA ANALYTICS
Agencies will sometimes in house data analytics for clients using proprietary tech, or the Agency is using a third party to run
the data analytics but the client is unable to pass the personal data directly to the third party.
In this situation Agency could be a Processor for two separate activities:
Firstly, if we are running in house analytics on the Personal Data then the Agency will be a Processor for the work carried out
on the Personal Data.
Secondly, If the Agency is simply passing the Personal Data over to a third party then the Agency is only Processing the
Personal Data when it is passed from the client to the third party.
Both activities will require a PPP

PRIVACY PROTECTION PROTOCOL
EVENTS
Some of our Agencies help clients with organising and running events. This situation going to be a lot less straightforward
then the three situations that have been detailed over the last three slides.
The main time Agency will be Processor will be for contact details of attendees, if Agency is keeping the contact list for the
client either because the Agency is advertising the event and collecting registration of attendees for the client or because we
will be helping to run email campaigns on attendees after the event has taken place.
A second less common situation is if we are running a booth or activity at an event that collects Personal Data from
attendees such as collecting additional contact information for a competition or photos of attendees.
Please Note: Because his situation is not as clear cut please consult the Legal and Privacy Teams when organising your
plans with the client.
